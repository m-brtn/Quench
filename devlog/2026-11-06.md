  - Two targets: `quench_audio` (headless: mixer, sounds, streams, WAV writer, signal analysis; also in the
    `headless` and `wasm-node` builds) and `quench_audio_device` (the miniaudio playback device and the iOS audio
    session; not in headless builds). One `quench_miniaudio` static library with the engine, node graph, resource
    manager and generation parts compiled out; device I/O compiled out in headless and web builds.
  - miniaudio at TrailForge's pin (`9634bed`, 0.11.25; shared dependencies keep the games' pins), public domain or
    MIT-0. Decoders WAV, FLAC and MP3 (built into miniaudio); no Vorbis (`stb_vorbis` is not needed by anything
    yet). On Apple platforms `MA_NO_RUNTIME_LINKING` with the frameworks linked (miniaudio's advice for
    notarization) and only the Core Audio and null backends compiled.
  - Mixer format: 32-bit float stereo at 48 kHz, 64-frame blocks; sources keep their rate and channel count
    (mono or stereo) and are resampled per voice (linear interpolation, 32.32 fixed-point position).
  - Gains, pitch and pan are smoothed per block (one-pole, 10 ms) and ramped linearly inside a block; stops and
    steals fade out over at least 3 ms; fades and crossfades use equal-power curves.
  - Attenuation models none, inverse (clamped), linear and exponential with min and max distance and rolloff;
    constant-power panning for mono sources, balance for stereo; Doppler from the radial velocities with a
    scale and the speed of sound (343 m/s); one listener.
  - Voice limit with priorities: a play beyond the limit steals the lowest-priority voice of at most the same
    priority (then the quietest, then the oldest); otherwise the new sound is rejected (reported as an end
    event).
  - Threads: the device callback thread is owned by miniaudio and the OS (documented as the one exception to the
    job-system rule). The game thread talks to it through single-producer single-consumer queues of plain
    commands and events and atomics for statistics; nothing is allocated, freed, locked or logged on the audio
    thread. Decoding and streaming run on engine jobs and work with zero workers.
  - Sounds are an asset type (`"sound"`, decoded to float PCM on a load job, CPU budget); a play of a sound
    that is still loading starts when it is ready (one-shots that would start more than 0.25 s late are
    dropped); a missing or corrupt sound plays silence and the asset manager logs it.
  - Streamed music reads the file in chunks through a new `quench::fs` reader (`fs::openAsset`), decoding on jobs
    into a ring of about one second per stream.
  - Flags `--no-audio` (offline mixing, output dropped), `--audio-device default|null|<name>` and
    `--audio-capture FILE.wav` (offline mixing on the frame clock, written at exit); scenario command
    `audio_measure` with RMS, peak and dominant frequency values. Tests and capture checks use the null backend
    or offline mixing: no sound from the Mac's speakers.
  - Audio cooking: `quench_cook sound` validates a WAV, FLAC or MP3 file (passes it through) or converts it to a
    16-bit WAV at another rate or channel count; `quench_cook_assets(... SOUNDS ...)`.
  - Demo sounds: generated at build time by a Python standard-library script (`tools/assets/make_demo_sounds.py`,
    procedural, CC0) and passed through `quench_cook`; no audio files in git except two tiny FLAC and MP3 test
    files for the decoders. Done 00:08.

- **03:05 — Shadow fix: cut-out and translucent casters (branch `fix/shadow-cutout-transparent`).** A separate
  git worktree from `5bace7f`; another agent works on stage 8 in the main checkout, which this work did not touch
  (no builds, no resets, no commits there). User report (2026-11-06, the demo's "Materials and lights" section on
  the iPhone, two screenshots): the perforated panel casts a solid shadow, its holes let no light through; the
  semi-transparent torus casts no shadow at all. Both causes are in `src/render/` (see "Problems and solutions").
  Decided without the user (away, "do not ask"), the recommended option each time:
  - `MaterialDesc::castsShadows` (bool) becomes `ShadowMode shadows = Auto`: `Auto` makes opaque materials solid
    casters, alpha-blended ones translucent and additive ones none; `None`, `Solid` and `Translucent` set it. A
    breaking change (allowed in 0.x; "Changes for games" below). `InstanceDesc::castsShadows` stays.
  - Cut-out shadows: a "mask" pipeline per surface (`surface_vs` with a new `surface_shadow_fs` that runs the surface
    function and discards below the cutoff, the same test as `surface_fs`). Only casters with `alphaCutoff > 0` use
    it; the rest keep the position-only pass.
  - Translucent shadows: a transmittance map per cascade, an Rgba16Float texture array at half the shadow map's
    size, made the first time a frame has a translucent caster. Its rgb multiplies the casters'
    `(1 - a) * lerp(1, hue, a)`, and its alpha keeps the largest `1 - depth`, the caster nearest the sun. Receivers
    behind that caster are lit through it, so the shadow is partial, smooth and tinted, and frames without
    translucent casters skip the pass. Rejected: dithering alone (grey and grainy; kept as the fallback), a colored
    map at full size (twice the depth array's memory), and depth peeling or deep shadow maps (more passes and memory
    than needed for one layer of glass).
  - Fallback by device support, not by platform: `TranslucentShadows::Dithered` where an Rgba16Float texture array
    cannot be rendered to and sampled (`DeviceSupport::transmittanceArray`): translucent casters are dithered into the
    depth map with a 4 x 4 Bayer pattern (`bayer4` in `quench/Common.hlsli`). `RenderSettings::translucentShadows`
    asks for it too.
  - Custom surfaces get optional `SurfaceDesc::shadowFragmentShader` and `transmittanceShader` (their
    `surface_shadow_fs` and `surface_transmittance_fs`, so a procedural opacity reaches the shadow too); without them

## Problems and solutions

- **`audio.stream_chunk_boundaries` failed to open its file.** The test helper wrote `music/loop.wav` without
  creating `music/`. It creates parent directories now.
- **`audio.decode_wav_flac_mp3`: 12672 MP3 frames for 11025 encoded.** miniaudio's dr_mp3 does not trim the LAME
  encoder delay and padding of this file. The test accepts up to three MP3 frames (3 × 576) more and says why; the
  docs say to loop WAV or FLAC.
- **Two test expectations were wrong**: the Doppler clamp compared a float with 4 exactly (now within 1e-5), and the
  stealing test played sounds of equal volume, so the quietest-first rule picked by start order (now distinct
  volumes, the quietest stolen first).
- **`dominantFrequency` read low levels between FFT bins.** The level did not correct for the Hann window's
  response off the bin center; it does now (`sin(x)/x/(1-o²)`), so `audio_freq_level` is about 1 for a full-scale
  sine at any frequency.
- **A stream that decodes to no frames played silence forever.** It now fails with "no audio frames".
- **The linker warned "ignoring duplicate libraries: libquench_audio.a"** for the demo, which linked `quench_audio`
  next to `quench_app` (which links it publicly). The demo no longer names it.
- **The demo's mix clipped** (peak 1.0): the motor, siren, music and the 40-ping burst together. Volumes lowered
  (motor 0.4, siren 0.6, music 0.3, burst pings 0.1); the scenario checks `audio_clipped == 0`.
- **The first Audio captures were hard to read**: the orbit ring and the fly-by lane lay on the ground while the
  emitters flew at ear height, the head's nose was hidden from behind, and the bus meters were below the panel's
  fold. Ring and lane moved to 1.2 m, ears added, the panel reordered (stats, buses with meters, music, emitters,
  distance).
- **The `wasm-node` build failed** with `-Wmissing-designated-field-initializers` (Emscripten's clang; Apple's clang
  does not warn): designated initializers of `PlayParams` and `MusicParams` skipped members without a default
  member initializer. Those members (`PlayParams::emitter`, `MusicParams::bus`, `BusDesc::name`,
  `DeviceDesc::name`) now have `{}`, so a game built with a newer clang does not get the warning either.
- **Every demo golden image changed** because the menu has a ninth line: see "Results".
- **hello's CPU p99 was 8.6 % worse** (5 + 5 runs; 5.0 % in 6 + 6 focused runs, CPU p50 +3.6 % with every run
  above every baseline run). Cause: the scenario monitor of the mix ran in every scripted run, also without
  `audio_measure`, and its per-frame copy into a 1.5 MB history ring missed the cache (about 6 µs at the median,
  20 µs at p99 of `updateAudio`). Solution: the monitor only for scenarios that use `audio_measure`, the copy in
  `memcpy` spans. What is left per frame with a device: about 4 µs at the median (queues, statistics, meters; not
  broken down further).
- **TrailForge's own miniaudio build would be linked next to the engine's** once it moves to 0.5.0. A link
  experiment (two static archives of `miniaudio.c` with the engine's and TrailForge's defines, a program calling
  the decoder through one and the encoder through the other) linked without a warning in both orders; with the
  engine's archive first, `-why_load` showed only TrailForge's object loaded (it alone has the encoder), so the
  engine-side code called TrailForge's configuration (no FLAC, other backends). Not a bug in the engine, but TrailForge must drop `miniaudio_lib`
  then (documented in `docs/dependencies.md` and below under "Changes for games").
- **The audio checks made `ctest` 4 to 5 s longer** (release 19.19 s, about 15 s before stage 7). Cause: 9.1 s of
  app time per backend in one scenario, at the display's 60 Hz with vsync (each frame is one 1/120 s step, so a
  scripted run goes at half real time). Solution: the split and `--present-mode immediate` (02:07 above); the frames
  then run at their cap of 120 per second with the same pixels and samples.
- **Stage 8: the first compute pipeline failed on Vulkan through MoltenVK** (`vkCreateComputePipeline
  VK_ERROR_INITIALIZATION_FAILED`; Metal passed). MoltenVK's MSL for argument buffers declared
  `device b_a* b_b`: glslang names the block type of a `StructuredBuffer<float4>` after its first variable (`b_a`),
  two buffers of one element type share that type, and the variable `b_a` hides the type's name. Solution:
  `quench_shaderc` appends `_block` to struct type names that another id also carries (debug names only; the code
  is unchanged), for every stage.
- **Stage 8: a cooked 90° linear rotation key pair lagged by 0.016 rad at a quarter** (`cook_animation_keys_and_
  settings`). Cause: glTF interpolates linear rotations spherically, ozz normalized-linearly. Solution: the cook
  splits steps wider than 0.2 rad with spherically interpolated keys (error below 1e-4 rad); ozz's key reduction
  drops the ones it does not need.
- **Stage 8: two `animation.*` crowd cases shared a scratch directory** and one aborted in `remove_all` when ctest ran
  them at once. Each case has its own directory now.
- **Stage 8: the demo asserted "asset type registered twice"** on start. Cause: `SceneAssets` registers the asset
  types "model" and "texture" on its manager, so one manager serves one `SceneAssets` (one scene), and the Assets
  section's scene already had the app's. Solution: the Animation section creates its own `AssetManager` on the
  app's job system (with the app's settings) and updates it each frame. The limit itself (stage 6 code) stays and is
  documented in `docs/assets.md`.
- **Stage 8: the slope stand's lower foot did not move down** (pelvis offset 0). Not a bug: the ramp's top was
  centered at floor height, so its lower half was under the floor, which the right foot stood on. The ramp now rises
  from the floor.
- **Stage 8: UBSan stopped every test with foot placement** ("signed integer overflow" in
  `simd_math_ref-inl.h:557`, from `IKTwoBoneJob`). Cause: on ARM ozz uses its scalar math, whose reciprocal square
  root estimate subtracts a float's bits as `int`; the two-bone IK runs it on all four lanes, and an unused lane
  holds a negative value (`Length3Sqr` fills the other lanes with x). The lane's result is not used, but the overflow
  is undefined behaviour. Solution: `quench_ozz` and every file that includes ozz's headers build with `-fwrapv`
  (`cmake/Dependencies.cmake`), which defines the wrap the trick assumes.
- **Stage 8: positional `MeshDesc{vertices, indices, name}` stopped compiling** when the skin fields were put before
  `name` (the engine's `MeshBuilder` and `SceneAssets` use it, games may too). The new fields come after `name` now.
- **The cut-out panel cast a solid shadow** (user report from the iPhone). Cause: the cascades drew every caster
  with `shadow_vs` (position only) and the empty `shadow_fs`, so neither texture alpha nor the cutoff reached the
  shadow. Solution: casters with `alphaCutoff > 0` go through a per-surface mask pipeline with
  `surface_shadow_fs`, which evaluates the surface and discards like `surface_fs`.
- **The semi-transparent torus cast no shadow** (user report). Cause: only `BlendMode::Opaque` materials were
  shadow caster candidates (`m.castsShadows && m.blend == BlendMode::Opaque`), and the demo's glass also had
  `castsShadows = false`. Solution: `ShadowMode`, whose `Auto` gives alpha-blended materials translucent shadows, and
  the transmittance maps (03:05).
- **Captures without translucent casters moved by a few levels** after the first version: `demo_shadows` up to 11
  levels, `demo_physics` 9 pixels up to 39, `demo_input` up to 4; all within the golden thresholds. Cause: the sun's
  radiance had become a variable that the transmittance branch may change. With it, the Metal compiler (fast math)
  ordered the direct light's products differently even when the branch was not taken. Experiments: restoring the
  old line in `surface_vs` changed nothing; restoring the old sun line made all three identical. Solution: two calls
  of `quenchDirectLight`, one per side of a `[branch]`, the side without translucent casters with the old
  expression (a comment says why). Afterwards all 20 other captures were bit for bit those of `5bace7f`.
- **A test lambda did not compile**: `REQUIRE` returns from a function that returns `glm::vec3`. It uses `CHECK` and
  returns a sentinel now.

## Limitations and open questions

- **Nothing was listened to.** Every check is numeric (levels, frequencies, counters, the largest step between
  samples). The tests, checks, benchmarks and smoke runs use the null backend or offline mixing, and
  `audio_device.default_output` (opt-in, `QUENCH_TEST_AUDIO_DEVICE=1`) was not run: Core Audio output itself is
  untested.
- **Not run on the iPhone** (the user has it): the stage 7 code, the audio session's activation, the Ring/Silent
  switch with the `Ambient` default, interruptions, route changes, background and foreground. In the iOS Simulator
  only setting and reading the category ran (`audio_device.audio_session_category`); the null device used there
  leaves the session alone.
- **Simplifications**: linear-interpolation resampling; stereo panning only (no HRTF, front and back alike, no
  occlusion, reverb or filters); one listener; no virtual voices (stolen and rejected sounds do not come back);
  stealing ranks by gain, not by the loudness of the samples; no limiter on the master bus (samples are clamped and
  counted). The cook converts channels and containers, not sample rates.
- **Formats**: WAV, FLAC, MP3 only; MP3 loops keep the encoder delay and padding.
- **Offline mode** (tests, `--audio-capture`, `--no-audio`, no device) decodes streams on the main thread.
- **Web**: the mixer runs under Node.js only; no browser output.
- **TrailForge** links its own miniaudio; moving it to 0.5.0 needs the change under "Changes for games".
- **`--present-mode immediate` was not paced to the display today**, unlike on 2026-11-05 (60 Hz and 15.5 ms of GPU
  time then): the hello benchmark with `immediate` ran 4286 frames in 10 s at 0.62 ms of GPU time on average (the
  display online at 60 Hz). Cause not known; `docs/platforms.md` and `docs/performance.md` now say both. The demo
  checks' shorter times depend on it: where `immediate` is paced, they take as long as with vsync and still check
  the same.
- **The mix checks run on Metal only**; Vulkan through MoltenVK writes the same WAV in the capture run
  (`demo_audio_wav_match`), not in the three mix scenarios.
- **Release 0.5.0** (02:23-02:28): released before anyone listened to the audio and before any iPhone run (decided
  without the user); the release notes say so under "What was not verified". The smoke log's first line is again
  the macOS message `sandbox_extension_issue_file_to_process failed ... (Operation not permitted)` for the app in
  the temporary unzip folder (as for 0.4.0); the run passed; not looked into.
- **Stage 8, not looked at in motion.** Foot sliding, blend pops, the cross-fades, the IK on the ramp and the step,
  the look-at and the held torch were checked by numbers (scenario values, test tolerances) and in still captures
  only; nobody watched the section run, and the footstep sound was not listened to.
- **Stage 8, not run on the iPhone**: the animation tests in the device test app, the demo's Animation section, the
  compute skinning on the A20 Pro and its GPU time, the animation benchmark region.
- **Stage 8, not done**: retargeting (clips bind to the skeleton they were cooked for; same joint names with other
  proportions need a rest-pose correction that ozz does not have), 2D blend spaces, sync markers beyond normalized
  time, animation LOD (every character is sampled and skinned every frame), dual quaternion skinning, blend shapes,
  more than four joints per vertex (`JOINTS_1` is dropped with a note), storage textures in compute.
- **Stage 8, simplifications**: foot placement casts one ray per leg (no prediction of the next footfall, no heel
  and toe); a cross-fade runs to its end before the next transition is checked; events come from states at a
  weight of 0.5 or more (one of a cross-fade's two); the skinning output buffers hold every drawn skinned vertex
  twice (this frame and the last); there is no CPU skinning fallback (every SDL GPU backend has compute).
- **Stage 8, content**: only the procedural mannequin has been animated; no character made in a DCC tool (Blender,
  Maya) was cooked, so glTF files with non-identity rest rotations, scaled joints or several skins ran only in the
  cook tests' small files.
- **Stage 8, one `SceneAssets` per `AssetManager`** (stage 6 design): the demo's Animation section has its own
  manager, so a file both scenes use would load twice. Left as it is; documented in `docs/assets.md`.
- **Stage 8, `-fwrapv`** is on for ozz and every engine file that includes its headers (`quench_animation`); it
  changed no capture (the Release captures after it equal the goldens made before it, largest difference 0) and
  its cost was not measured on its own.
- **Shadow fix, not verified on the iPhone** (no `devicectl` runs in this work): the user's report came from the
  iPhone, and the fix was seen only in Mac captures (Metal, Vulkan through MoltenVK) and built for iOS
  (Simulator tests). DXIL is built, never run.
- **The dithered fallback** is never chosen automatically on the Mac or in the Simulator (both render and sample
  Rgba16Float arrays). It was tested only by asking for it (`RenderSettings::translucentShadows`, the demo's
  `set translucent_shadows dithered`) and through `selectEffects` with a device that lacks the format
  (`render.effects_fall_back_by_device_support`).
- **Translucent shadows are a simplification**: one layer per texel. Overlapping translucent casters multiply their
  color, but the depth is that of the one nearest the sun, so a receiver between two translucent casters takes both
  tints. There is no thickness or absorption by distance, and a closed glass object attenuates once per face toward
  the sun. The map has half the shadow map's resolution (24 MiB at the default 2048 and 3 cascades), so its edges
  are softer than the depth shadow's. Receivers less than 2 transmittance texels behind a caster are not tinted, and
  the tint is full from 4 texels.
- **Motion vectors of moving cut-out objects** (found while checking the passes that draw the same geometry; it was
  there before this change and is not changed by it): the velocity pass draws a moved instance's full triangles, so
  its holes take the object's motion, and under TAA the background seen through them may smear. Recorded in
  `docs/render.md`; not fixed (it needs the surface in the velocity pass).
- **Benchmarks** ran on a busy Mac (another agent working at the same time); the spread between runs was larger
  than any difference between the builds (see "Results").
- **Merge with stage 8**: the branch is based on `5bace7f`; stage 8 (GPU skinning in `quench_render`) is likely to
  touch the same files (`src/render/Renderer.cpp`, `include/quench/render/Renderer.h` and `Scene.h`,
  `shaders/quench/SceneUniforms.h`, `Surface.hlsli`, `SurfaceEntries.hlsli`, `CMakeLists.txt`'s render shader
  entries, `tests/CMakeLists.txt`, `tests/render/RendererTests.cpp`, `AGENTS.md`'s current-state paragraph, this
  devlog). The mask and transmittance pipelines use the surface's
  own vertex shader in its shadow mode (`DrawUniforms::info.z` 1, the cascade's matrix), so a deformation in
  `surface_vs` reaches them; skinning there has to keep that mode, and plain skinned casters still need
  `shadowVertexShader`.
- **Merged with stage 8 (04:46)**: skinning runs in a compute pass before the frame, so no vertex shader has to
  deform; every shadow path binds the skinned vertex buffer instead (above). A custom surface's `surface_vs` on a
  skinned instance reads the skinned vertices in every pass. Not run on the iPhone either.
- **The translucent shadows cost about 0.1 ms of CPU per frame** in the materials section (three transmittance
  passes, one per cascade, one draw each): +7 to +9 % CPU p50 against `5bace7f`, over the 5 % rule, explained in
  `docs/performance.md` and kept. A cascade without translucent casters already opens no pass (the renderer's
  `transmittanceMask`); here the glass is in all three. Not tried: several cascades in one pass. The GPU cost of
  the shadow passes is not measured on its own.
