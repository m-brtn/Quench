# Quench 0.8.0

2026-11-06. Stage 10 of the [roadmap](../roadmap.md): the finished engine demo, and what it needed from the engine,
added for any game. Source only. Changes since [0.7.0](v0.7.0.md) (tag `v0.7.0`). With it the plan for stages 6 to
10 has been worked through; what stays open is in the roadmap's [What is next](../roadmap.md#what-is-next). Everything
here ran on the Mac and in the iOS Simulator only: nothing of 0.4.0 to 0.8.0 has run on an iPhone (see "What was not
verified").

## What changed

- **The engine demo, finished** ([Engine demo](../demo.md)). Each section is a file of its own under
  `examples/demo/sections/` behind one `Section` interface, and the app around them is split by concern (`Demo.cpp`,
  `Ui.cpp`, `Camera.cpp`, `Modes.cpp`, `Checks.cpp`, `Settings.cpp`). Every section's panel has the same parts: its
  controls, its numbers, "Frame and fallbacks" (the scene's counts, what runs after the device fallbacks, every
  fallback with its reason, the GPU backend and its capabilities) and the reset buttons; the description of what to
  look at is a caption under the scene. One camera control for every 3D section: mouse and keyboard, gamepad, touch
  (one finger orbits, two pan and pinch); the menu also by keyboard (Tab, the arrows, Enter) and by gamepad (the
  D-pad, A, B). The scenes themselves did not change.
- **Two layouts.** Wide for desktop windows and tablets; compact for phones and small windows: a bar at the top
  (the menu, the previous and next section, the section's controls) and one sheet at a time, docked at the bottom
  in portrait and on the right in landscape. Sizes follow the display's scale, windows stay inside the safe area, and
  touch-sized controls are on where the device has a touch screen (a setting: auto, on, off).
- **Modes.** A tour through the sections (`--tour [SECONDS]`); a check mode (`--checks`) over the 24 checks that
  wait for a person (looks and listening, each set up in its section; OK, problem or skip with a note, written at
  once to `checks.json` in the user directory and kept across launches); a benchmark of every section
  (`--benchmark-all [SECONDS]`) into one `benchmark.json`. Also `--no-settings`, `--settings-file`,
  `--checks-file`, `--benchmark-file`.
- **About and settings.** About shows the version, the build type and commit, the platform, the GPU backend, driver,
  device and capabilities, the window, the display's headroom, the audio device, the job threads, the controls and
  the licences (shipped as assets); on the Mac a button starts the demo again with the other GPU backend. Settings
  (UI scale, touch controls, HDR, present mode, the tour's time) are versioned JSON with the defaults for a file that
  cannot be read; scripted runs ignore them. The Performance panel gained a frame-time graph, the process's memory,
  the live GPU resources, the section load and switch times and a job test; a Log panel shows the demo's log lines.
- **Checks.** New CTest tests: `demo_compact_portrait_metal` and `demo_compact_landscape_metal` (golden images of
  the compact layout), `demo_tour`, `demo_checks`, `demo_settings` (a fresh user directory each, the JSON read back),
  `demo_benchmark`, `demo_bad_flag`, and `demo_cycle`, a leak test: every section three times (switched in, again,
  then reset) against the marks after the first round. 385 tests in all. The demo's 25 golden images changed only
  where the windows are.
- **New in `quench_app`, for any game** ([App shell](../app.md)): `App::beginPerf(label)`, `perfRunning()` and
  `endPerf()`, a perf region started by the game as a scenario's `perf_begin` .. `perf_end`, returning a
  `PerfReport` (frames; frame, CPU and GPU times; the GPU time per timed range; the game's details);
  `App::arguments()`, `canRelaunch()` and `relaunch(args)` (desktops: the program starts again as a new process,
  this one ends after the frame); `App::memoryFootprint()` (Apple platforms; nullopt elsewhere); the flags
  `--orientation portrait|landscape|any` (phones and tablets, within the Info.plist's orientations) and
  `--user-dir DIR`; the scenario values `memory_mb` and `gpu_resources`.
- **Also new:** `gpu::backendAvailable(Backend)`; `render::Renderer::fallbacks()` (the fallback warnings as lines for
  a game's UI); `quench_cook_assets(... FILES <file>...)`, files copied as they are (a call with `FILES` only needs no
  `quench_cook`, also in cross builds without `host-tools`).
- **Changed:** `quench::app::Input` no longer reports a Mac trackpad's touches as fingers; the trackpad still moves
  the mouse (as fingers they turned the demo's camera a second time).
- **Fixed:** the ray tracing tables of a new scene whose meshes reuse an old scene's handles.
- **Packaging:** `tools/package_demo.sh --ios` builds the demo's assets before the app and fails when the app's
  `assets/` differs from the staged folder: one Xcode build had left the two licence files out of the app (not
  reproduced in six more builds; the cause is unknown).

## What was verified

From the [devlog of 2026-11-06](../../devlog/2026-11-06.md). Mac (Apple M4 Pro) and the iOS Simulator only.

- At the release commit: format check clean; `debug` and `release` build without warnings, ctest 385 tests each,
  367 passed, 18 skipped (the same 18 as in 0.7.0: the ray tracing and MetalFX cases on SDL GPU and on Vulkan
  through MoltenVK, which have neither, and the two audio device cases), among them the demo's checks on Metal, on
  Vulkan through MoltenVK with the validation layers and on the native backend; `asan` 385 tests with the same 18
  skipped, without an AddressSanitizer or UndefinedBehaviorSanitizer report; `host-tools`, `ios-sim-release` and
  `ios-device-release` without warnings; `wasm-node` under Node.js 113 tests, 106 passed, 7 skipped (the cases that
  need threads); in the iOS Simulator (iPhone 18 Pro Max, iOS 27.0) `ios-sim-tests` 263 tests, 241 passed, 22
  skipped (ray tracing and MetalFX, which the Simulator lacks, and the window, headroom and opt-in device output
  cases); `tools/new_game.py`: the generated game builds against this checkout, starts and quits; `--version`
  prints 0.8.0; captures looked at.
- On the code of this release before the release commit (which adds only the version and documentation):
  `tools/package_demo.sh --ios` ran the demo's 42 checks and the smoke run from a plain `unzip`, and the macOS app
  and the iOS `.ipa` held `LICENSE` and `THIRD_PARTY_LICENSES`. `tsan` was not run (no change to the job system or
  to threaded engine code).
- The scenes did not change: with the windows hidden, the Release demo's 25 section captures are identical to
  0.7.0's (mean difference 0.0000, largest pixel difference 0), and its three audio WAVs are byte-identical. The
  golden images changed only where the windows are (mean difference 7.83 to 19.84, all of it inside
  x 7..951, y 26..530 of 960 x 540); both were looked at side by side.
- `demo_cycle`: after three rounds through every section, GPU resources within 3 of the marks after the first
  round, memory less than 32 MB above them, the same scene instances, no new error. A section's first visit costs
  0.3 to 3.3 ms in Release (its assets then load on the job system). The compact layout was looked at in the iOS
  Simulator in portrait and landscape; the relaunch with the other backend ran from a script on the Mac.
- Performance, Release with vsync at about 120 Hz, 1920 x 1080, medians in ms ([Performance](../performance.md)).
  hello against 0.7.0 in 12 interleaved rounds: CPU p50 0.295 -> 0.295 (+0.0 %), p99 0.508 -> 0.514 (+1.2 %), GPU
  p50 0.555 -> 0.566; hello's per-frame code did not change. The demo's benchmark regions with the windows shown:
  CPU p50 0.01 to 0.12 ms more (+0.4 % in the animation region to +12.6 % in the assets region), the cost of the
  new panels in ImGui; with the windows hidden the regions scatter around zero. GPU times vary both ways.

## What was not verified

- Nothing of 0.4.0 to 0.8.0 has run on an iPhone: not the compact layout, the touch controls, the safe area or the
  orientations on a device, not the check mode or the benchmark mode there, and none of the earlier items (the
  asset system, audio and the audio session, animation, the native backend, ray tracing and MetalFX, 120 Hz on the
  native backend).
- Nobody has used the demo by hand since its stage 10 changes: the tour, the check mode, the benchmark mode, the
  settings and the layouts ran from scripts; the relaunch button was pressed by a script; a real gamepad and the
  trackpad were not tried. Nothing was listened to, and nothing was watched in motion.
- `App::relaunch` ran on macOS only; `App::memoryFootprint` is implemented for Apple platforms only.
- Why one Xcode build left files out of the app's assets is not known; a game's own Xcode build has no check for
  it.
- Carried over from 0.7.0: HDR output on an HDR display (this Mac's has no HDR range), ray tracing on Vulkan
  (MoltenVK has none), DXIL of the ray query entries (not built on the Mac).

## Changes for games

What a game on 0.7.0 changes or checks when it moves to 0.8.0. Nothing was renamed or removed.

- **`--orientation` and `--user-dir` are engine flags now:** a game flag with one of these names no longer reaches
  `createGame`.
- **`memory_mb` and `gpu_resources` are engine scenario values now:** a game value of the same name is no longer
  read (the engine's values come first).
- **`Input::fingers()` no longer reports a Mac trackpad's touches.** The trackpad still moves the mouse; a game that
  read its touches as fingers on a Mac loses them.
- New in `quench_app`: `App::beginPerf(label)`, `perfRunning()`, `endPerf()` and `PerfReport`; one region at a time,
  and a new one from either side (the game or a scenario's `perf_begin`) ends the running one without a report.
  `App::arguments()`, `canRelaunch()`, `relaunch(args)` (desktops only). `App::memoryFootprint()`.
- New: `gpu::backendAvailable(Backend)`; `render::Renderer::fallbacks()`.
- Fixed: the ray tracing tables of a new scene whose meshes reuse an old scene's handles.
- CMake: `quench_cook_assets(... FILES <file>...)` copies files as they are; a call with `FILES` only needs no
  `quench_cook`.
- `AudioSystem::setMonitor` may be called at any time (its comment said otherwise; the code did not change).
- A game moves its FetchContent pin to the commit of `v0.8.0`.

## Known limitations

- The demo's panels cost about 0.05 to 0.16 ms of CPU per frame with the windows shown (ImGui); a game pays nothing
  for them.
- The check mode's 24 items are written into the demo (`examples/demo/Checks.cpp`), from the checks that waited for
  a person at 0.7.0; a new check needs a code change.
- iOS cannot start an app again from inside: there the GPU backend is chosen at launch (`--gpu-backend metal` or
  `QUENCH_GPU_BACKEND=metal`).
- Carried over: ray tracing covers sun shadows and ambient occlusion only (no reflections or indirect light, local
  lights unshadowed), no bottom-level compaction or top-level refit; MetalFX temporal costs about 2.1 ms at a 1080p
  output on the M4 Pro; the native backend's timestamps cover the frame's main command buffer only. Stage 6's shader
  hot reload, ASTC and Basis Universal textures, normal maps in the standard surface and asset hot reload on a
  device; stage 7's browser audio, HRTF, reverb and Vorbis; stage 8's retargeting, 2D blend spaces, animation LOD,
  dual quaternion skinning and blend shapes. The demo's macOS package is signed ad hoc and not notarized; its iOS
  package is development-signed and runs only on the devices of its profile. Windows, Linux, Android and browsers
  have not been run.
