# Quench 0.10.0

2026-11-07. Stage 12 of the [roadmap](../roadmap.md): area lights (rect, disk and tube lights with LTC shading,
soft ray-traced shadows and path tracer sampling), anisotropic GGX and the demo's tunnel in motion. With it, the
remaining work of earlier stages: ASTC textures for iOS, asset hot reload on a device and shader hot reload on
desktops (stage 6); Ogg Vorbis, reverb buses and HRTF (stage 7); 2D blend spaces, animation LOD, dual quaternion
skinning, retargeting at cook time and blend shapes with weights from clips (stage 8); normal maps in ray hits, the
probe grid traced in turns, shadow maps for spot and point lights, and GTAO and SSR as screen-space fallbacks
(stages 9 and 11; every one off by default); and a round of fixes. Source only. Changes since [0.9.0](v0.9.0.md)
(tag `v0.9.0`). Everything was tested on the Mac (Apple M4 Pro) and in the iOS Simulator; on an iPhone 17 only the
tunnel, a ten-minute run of the phone defaults, ASTC textures and asset hot reload ran. Nobody has looked at or
listened to the new features in motion (see "What was not verified").

## What changed

### Rendering

- **Area lights** in `quench_render`: rect, disk and tube lights, shaded by linearly transformed cosines in the
  raster and the ray hits, with soft ray-traced shadows, sampled by the path tracer, their shapes drawn in the scene
  ([Render module](../render.md#area-lights)). The fitted LTC table and integrals come from selfshadow/ltc_code
  (BSD-2-Clause; `THIRD_PARTY_LICENSES`, [Dependencies](../dependencies.md)). Without ray tracing they are
  shaded without shadows.
- **Anisotropic GGX** in the standard surface, the ray hits and the path tracer
  ([Render module](../render.md#anisotropic-ggx)).
- **Surface shader variants:** `surface_basic_fs`, the surface shader without the area light and anisotropy paths,
  draws materials without anisotropy in frames without area lights. Without it the standard surface was 5 to 8 %
  slower in such frames than before stage 12 (unpaced, M4 Pro); with it within 0.6 %
  ([Performance](../performance.md#the-tunnel)).
- **Normal maps in ray hits and the path tracer** (stage 11 and 12 remaining work): traced reflections, indirect
  light, the probe grid and the path tracer read a material's normal map (texture 2) in the raster's tangent frame
  ([Render module](../render.md#ray-traced-lighting)). In `renderer_ray_hits_read_normal_maps` a wall whose map
  turns it toward the sun is 4.07 times as bright as a plain one in the raster, 3.99 times in its traced mirror
  image and 3.80 times in the path tracer (1.00 and 0.97 before). The tracing passes have variants with the normal
  maps, used only in frames where an instance has one: frames without normal maps cost what they did (the demo's
  ray tracing section, offscreen at 1080p on the M4 Pro: reflections 0.901 / 0.903 ms, the path tracer 12.70 /
  12.72 ms); in the tunnel, which has them, reflections cost 0.33 ms (5 %) and the path tracer 1.2 ms (4 %) more.
- **The probe grid in turns, for phones** (stage 11 remaining work): `RenderSettings::probeUpdatePeriod` (1, 2, 4
  or 8, default 1) traces that fraction of the 2048 probes per frame, every probe once in n frames, spread evenly
  over the grid ([Render module](../render.md#ray-traced-lighting)). Probes that come into a scrolling grid are left
  out of the shading until their turn (irradiance alpha 0), and a grid that jumps traces every probe. On the M4 Pro
  (offscreen, 1080p, native backend) the probes cost 1.16 ms every frame and 0.68, 0.43 and 0.32 ms in 2, 4 and 8
  turns; light settles to within 10 % of a change in 70, 166, 276 and 629 frames
  ([Performance](../performance.md#probe-grid-in-turns)). The default frame is the one before, bit for bit; the
  `probes` range reads 0.01 ms more. The demo's Graphics panel has "Probe updates" (`set probe_updates`);
  `scenarios/raytracing_phone_probes.txt` measures it on a phone (not run on one yet).
- **Shadow maps for spot and point lights** (stage 9 and 11 remaining work): where local light shadows cannot be
  ray traced (SDL GPU, Vulkan through MoltenVK, the native backend without ray tracing),
  `RenderSettings::localShadowMapSize` (off by default; 64..4096) gives the same `localLightShadows` lights shadow
  maps: one per spot light under 60 degrees, six per point light or wider spot, filtered 4 x 4
  ([Render module](../render.md#local-light-shadow-maps)). Area lights stay unshadowed there. The lookup is in a
  surface variant of its own (`surface_local_shadows_fs`), so frames without maps cost what they did (the M4 Pro,
  offscreen at 1080p: `hdr` within 0.3 % on the native backend in four sections, SDL GPU's frame and CPU times
  within noise); the demo's ray tracing section's two lamps (7 maps of 512) cost 0.3 to 0.4 ms of the frame on SDL
  GPU ([Performance](../performance.md#local-light-shadow-maps)). In `renderer_local_light_shadow_maps` the shadowed
  points read 0 where the open ground reads 375 to 687 and the open ground matches the unshadowed image (no acne or
  seams). The demo's Graphics panel and the ray tracing section have "Shadow maps" (`set local_shadow_maps`), and
  the batches a part `raytracing_maps` with new golden images for Metal and Vulkan through MoltenVK.
- **Screen-space ambient occlusion, GTAO** (stage 9 and 11 remaining work): `AmbientOcclusion::ScreenSpace`, and
  `RenderSettings::screenSpaceFallbacks` (off by default), which turns the traced occlusion into GTAO where the
  device cannot trace (SDL GPU, Vulkan through MoltenVK, the native backend without ray tracing)
  ([Render module](../render.md#screen-space-ambient-occlusion)). Adapted from XeGTAO's main pass (MIT, Intel;
  `THIRD_PARTY_LICENSES`, [Dependencies](../dependencies.md)) at half resolution, with a depth-aware 4 x 4 blur and
  upsample into the mask the surfaces already read for the traced occlusion, after a depth prepass of its own where
  nothing is traced. On the M4 Pro (offscreen, 1080p) 0.41 ms in the demo's shadows section and 0.17 ms in the ray
  tracing section on the native backend; frames without it cost what they did
  ([Performance](../performance.md#screen-space-ambient-occlusion)). The demo's Graphics panel has "Screen space
  (GTAO)" and "Screen-space fallbacks" (`set ao screen`, `set screen_fallbacks`), the ray tracing section the
  fallback without ray tracing, and the batches a part `raytracing_screen` with new golden images for Metal and
  Vulkan through MoltenVK; `scenarios/gtao_phone.txt` measures it on a phone (not run on one yet).
- **Screen-space reflections, SSR** (stage 9 and 11 remaining work): `Reflections::ScreenSpace`, and
  `RenderSettings::screenSpaceFallbacks` (the same flag), which now also turns the traced reflections into SSR where
  the device cannot trace ([Render module](../render.md#screen-space-reflections)). A ray per 2 x 2 pixels along
  the GGX lobe of the G-buffer's normal and roughness, steps relative to the view depth, the hit's color from the
  previous frame's image, a depth-aware resolve with Karis weights, after a prepass with the G-buffer where nothing
  is traced. On the M4 Pro (offscreen, 1080p) on the native backend 0.12 ms in the demo's materials section and
  0.18 ms in the shadows section (the frame 0.27 and 0.32 ms more, with the prepass and the copy of the image),
  0.70 ms in the tunnel in place of 3.65 ms of traced reflections; on SDL GPU about 1.9 ms in the tunnel; frames
  without it cost what they did ([Performance](../performance.md#screen-space-reflections)). The demo's Graphics
  panel has "Screen space (SSR)" (`set reflections screen`), the screen-space fallbacks cover it, the batches'
  `raytracing_screen` part shows it (the gold spheres reflect the floor), and `scenarios/ssr_phone.txt` measures it
  on a phone (not run on one yet).
- **Statistics and timing:** `RenderStats::areaLights` and `localShadowChanges` (the lights that took another
  light's ray-traced shadow channel this frame). The renderer's `accel` timing range has two ranges inside it:
  `blas` (the bottom levels' builds, refits and compactions; absent in a frame without them) and `tlas` (the top
  level's build or refit).

### The demo

- **Section 12, "Tunnel"** ([Engine demo](../demo.md#sections)): eight area lights and two spots, metal panels, wet
  asphalt with a puddle, rubber, embossed plastic and brushed aluminium from textures written at build time; since
  the user's request after stage 12 the tunnel is in motion: moving and turning lamps with their fixtures, a camera
  that drives a loop through the tunnel, a pause and a free camera. Phones that trace run it with two lamp shadows
  and no traced reflections at half size with MetalFX spatial; phones without ray tracing render it at three
  quarters of the size; every phone at 60 Hz ([Performance](../performance.md#the-tunnel-in-motion)).
- **New views and options** for the features below: the Animation section's "2D blend space", "Skinning",
  "Retargeting" and "Blend shapes" views and its animation LOD option, the audio section's reverb and HRTF switches,
  the Graphics panel's probe updates, shadow maps and screen-space fallbacks; each with a scripted check and golden
  captures where it draws something, the animation views and the audio switches also with check mode items.
- The switch cycle checks the memory to below 64 MB of growth (was 32): the footprint moves within about 60 MB from
  round to round on the native backend. It now also runs on Vulkan through MoltenVK.

### Assets and the cook (stage 6 remaining work)

- **ASTC 4x4 textures:** `quench_cook texture --format astc` encodes them through astc-encoder 5.7.1 (Apache-2.0,
  in the cook only), `quench_cook_assets(TEXTURE_FORMAT auto)` cooks ASTC in iOS builds and BC7 elsewhere, and
  `SceneAssets` samples them where `gpu::Caps::astcTextures` is set ([Assets](../assets.md#cooked-formats)). PSNR
  54 to 57 dB on the cook's test images (BC7: 44 to 51 dB); the demo's Assets section ran with them in the iOS
  Simulator and on the iPhone 17.
- **Asset hot reload on a device:** development builds read assets from `asset_overlay/` in the user directory
  first (`fs::makeOverlayBackend`; CMake option `QUENCH_ASSET_OVERLAY`, on without `NDEBUG`), and
  `tools/push_assets.sh` copies files whose content changed into it with `devicectl`
  ([Assets](../assets.md#hot-reload-on-a-device)). On the iPhone 17 a pushed texture was reloaded about a second
  later. The demo's Assets section turns hot reload on with `--hot-reload` (before, only its switch did).

### App shell and GPU layer

- **Shader hot reload on desktops** (stage 6 remaining work): with `--hot-reload` an edit of an HLSL source or of
  a file it includes is compiled again with the build's commands (`SDL_CreateProcess`, a manifest per set from
  `quench_add_shaders()`), and the pipelines that use the set are created again behind the same handles; after a
  compile error the old code stays ([App shell](../app.md#shader-hot-reload)). `gpu::Device::enableShaderHotReload`,
  `pollShaderHotReload` and `shaderReloadStatus`; scenario values `shader_reloads`, `shader_reload_failures`,
  `shader_reload_pipelines`. Checked on the Mac's three backends; the renderer's `render_frame` set (15 entries)
  compiled again in 3.7 to 4.2 s while the demo kept rendering.
- **Perf regions report more:** the process's CPU time per frame and the share of it on performance cores (Apple
  platforms; `perf_process_cpu`, `perf_cpu_performance_share`, on the `perf:` line and in the demo's
  `benchmark.json`): the frame's CPU time read 0.29 or 1.2 ms for the same work on the M4 Pro, by where the
  scheduler ran it ([Performance](../performance.md#cpu-time-and-the-cores)). Also the frames' wait for the
  swapchain texture (offscreen, for the oldest frame in flight), the present mode in use and the display's rate, so
  a run with `--present-mode immediate` shows whether it was paced ([Performance](../performance.md#the-benchmark)).
- **Offscreen perf regions time GPU-bound frames on the GPU** (before, they had no GPU sample and no times per
  range); unpaced offscreen A/B runs of GPU costs need no window now.

### Audio (stage 7 remaining work)

- **Ogg Vorbis sounds and streams:** `quench_audio` decodes `.ogg` files through stb_vorbis 1.22 from miniaudio's
  archive (MIT or public domain; no new download), as sounds in memory, streams and music, and `quench_cook sound`
  and `quench_cook_assets(SOUNDS)` accept them ([Audio](../audio.md#streams-and-music)). A streamed Vorbis loop needs
  no length (miniaudio reports none for it there) and gives exactly the frames of the file decoded in memory every
  time round; the test loop decodes to its 48000 frames at 38 to 39 dB signal to error against its source; also
  under Node.js.
- **Reverb buses with sends:** `BusDesc::reverb` makes a bus a Freeverb reverb (verblib 0.5 from miniaudio's
  archive, MIT-0 or public domain; no new download) whose output replaces its input; sounds send to it with
  `PlayParams::sendBus` and `sendLevel` or `AudioSystem::setSend`, and `setReverb` changes the room size, damping
  and width ([Audio](../audio.md#reverb)). Rendered on the audio thread without allocating (counted: 0 in 600
  renders), bit for bit the dry mix when nothing reaches it, idle under -100 dB; RT60 0.67, 1.08 and 2.43 s at room
  sizes 0.2, 0.5 and 0.8; about 1 µs per millisecond of audio for one reverb on the M4 Pro. The demo's audio section
  has a reverb switch and a room size slider, a mix check (`audio_reverb.txt`) and a check mode item for listening.
- **HRTF for headphones:** with `AudioSettings::hrtf` or `AudioSystem::setHrtf(true)` spatial sounds are filtered by
  head-related impulse responses instead of panned, at most `AudioSettings::hrtfVoices` (8) at once, the others
  panned; switching crossfades over 10 ms ([Audio](../audio.md#hrtf)). The responses are SADIE's KU100 set as
  Resonance Audio distributes it (Apache-2.0; `THIRD_PARTY_LICENSES`), reduced by `tools/make_hrtf_table.py` to 386
  directions of 128 taps with per-ear delays (`src/audio/HrtfTable.inc`, 198 KB in the binary), interpolated
  between directions and convolved directly on the audio thread without allocating. Noise 2 m to the side: 15.4 dB
  between the ears and 39 frames of delay, as in the source data; bit for bit the panned mix when off; about 1.2 µs
  per HRTF voice and millisecond of audio on the M4 Pro (32 voices through it: 4 % of a core). The demo's audio
  section has an HRTF switch, a mix check (`audio_hrtf.txt`) and a check mode item for listening in headphones.

### Animation (stage 8 remaining work)

- **2D blend spaces:** a graph state's `blend2d` blends up to 16 clips placed at points of two float parameters,
  weighted by gradient band interpolation (freeform Cartesian), the clips in phase with a per-point `phase` offset;
  also in 1D trees ([Animation](../animation.md#graphs-state-machines)). On a grid of the demo's six points the
  weights stay in 0..1, sum to 1 within 1.2e-7, change by at most 0.0018 per mm and are mirror symmetric. The demo's
  mannequin has walk back and strafe clips, and its Animation section a view "2D blend space" with a character
  steered by its velocity while facing one way (`animation_blend2d.txt`: its root motion within 0.3 to 11 degrees
  of the asked direction).
- **Animation LOD:** `AnimationSystem::setLod` and `setLodView` sample the clips of characters small on the screen
  every 2nd or 4th frame (thresholds of screen size with hysteresis, staggered over the frames), interpolated
  between updates (computed a period ahead, so not late) or held, with foot placement and look-at off below a size,
  root motion spread over the frames ([Animation](../animation.md#animation-lod)). Off by default; with it off the
  update is bit for bit what it was. The renderer copies the skinned vertices of an instance whose palette did not
  change instead of skinning it again (`RenderStats::skinnedReused`). On the demo's crowd of 256 (M4 Pro,
  offscreen) from 45 m: 65 of 260 characters sampled per frame; the update 0.31 ms on one thread, 0.26
  interpolated, 0.14 held; the skinning pass 1.75 ms GPU, 0.46 held.
- **Dual quaternion skinning:** `render::Skinning::DualQuaternion` per instance (`InstanceDesc::skinning`,
  `Scene::setSkinning`) or per skin of a cooked model (`SceneAssets::setSkinning`), linear the default
  ([Animation](../animation.md#gpu-skinning)). A bar twisted by 180 degrees keeps its full width (11 of 11 pixels at
  the middle, linear 0); rigid motions and translations render as with linear skinning. On the demo's crowd of 256
  (M4 Pro, offscreen) it costs 0.06 ms of frame CPU and nothing measurable on the GPU.
- **Retargeting at cook time:** `quench_cook animation IN OUT --skeleton TARGET --retarget MAP.json` and
  `quench_cook_assets(... ANIMATIONS ... SKELETON target.gltf RETARGET map.json)` (output `<path>.<target>.qanim`)
  cook clips for a skeleton with other joint names, proportions and rest pose: a JSON bone map by name, the
  source's model-space rotations with the rest poses and the bones' directions corrected, the root's and hips'
  translations scaled by the hips' height ratio (or a given scale); unmapped joints keep their rest
  ([Animation](../animation.md#retargeting)). The demo's mannequin's walk on its second character (legs 1.15 times
  as long, other names, a T-pose, an extra spine joint), through the runtime: feet within 5.1 mm of 1.15 times the
  mannequin's in world space, hands within 0.8 mm of 1.1 times from the shoulders, planted feet moving at most
  0.46 mm per frame (the mannequin's 0.44 mm).
- **Blend shapes:** glTF morph targets (position, normal and tangent offsets, names from `extras.targetNames`,
  default weights from the node or mesh) are cooked into `.qmodel` version 4 and applied by the renderer's skinning
  pass before the joints, also for meshes without joints ([Render module](../render.md#blend-shapes),
  [Assets](../assets.md#cooked-formats)). A game sets the weights per instance (`Scene::setMorphWeights`) or by name
  for a cooked model (`SceneAssets::setMorphWeights`). With every weight 0 the image is the one of the mesh without
  blend shapes, bit for bit; with weights (also negative and above 1) the one of the mesh morphed on the CPU, 0
  pixels more than 2 levels apart, on Metal, Vulkan through MoltenVK and the native backend, also before linear and
  dual quaternion skinning; motion vectors, culling bounds and ray-traced shadows follow the weights.
- **Blend shape weights from clips:** clips carry named float channels (`ClipDesc::channels`, ozz float tracks),
  the cook turns glTF `weights` animations into them (one per target, named like the model's blend shapes), and
  `AnimationSystem::addChannels` / `channels` give a game their values after each update, blended like the pose
  (blend trees and spaces, cross-fades, override and additive layers, animation LOD; a clip without a channel
  leaves its weight to the rest value). Checked by numbers: exact at and between keys, the blends' expected values
  within 1e-5, a newer file version refused; cooked linear, step and cubic weights through the runtime within 3e-3;
  LOD interpolation within 1e-5 of an update every frame. The demo's blob takes its sway clip's squash and stretch.

### Fixes

- **`JobSystem::wait()` could return before the job left `pendingCount()`:** the job was marked done before it was
  counted out, so a `pendingCount()` right after waiting for every job read 1 now and then (`core.jobs_run_and_wait`
  under ThreadSanitizer and load: 13 of 480 runs). `wait()` now returns once the job is also counted out; 0 of 1200
  runs since.
- **Memory growth on SDL GPU's Vulkan driver:** a fence released after it signalled could keep its command buffer,
  and every texture and buffer it used, in flight for good (a reference counting issue in SDL's Vulkan driver), so
  cycled render targets were made anew every frame: the demo's assets section reached 4.5 GB on Vulkan through
  MoltenVK. The GPU layer now waits on a signalled fence (which does not block) before it releases it: 676 MB, flat.
  The demo's switch cycle grows by 34 MB in its third round on Vulkan, where it grew by 459 MB before.
- **Perf regions count each frame's GPU time once, in its own region:** before, every frame repeated the last GPU
  time read (a frame read late, twice, or one of the previous region's). New values `perf_gpu_samples` and
  `perf_gpu_lost`; hello's batches check that no timed frame is lost.
- **Perf regions count each frame's time in its own region:** a frame's time (from its start to the next frame's
  start) went into the region of the next frame, so a region's first sample was the frame before it: a capture
  just before `perf_begin` put its PNG encoding into the region (708 ms on the iPhone 17, 610 ms on the M4 Pro at
  1206 x 2622), and the region's last frame was not counted. The capture still stalls its own frame.
- **Motion vectors through the holes of a moving cut-out object:** its motion covered its holes, so TAA, the
  temporal denoiser and MetalFX reprojected what is seen through them with the object's motion. Moving cut-out
  instances now write their motion only where the frame's depth is their own (`velocity_cutout_fs`, without a depth
  target): the motion vector in a hole of a panel moving 0.1 m per frame read 251 of 255 in a temporary view of the
  motion vectors before, 0 after, on all three backends.
- **Jitter and noise restart at a camera cut:** the TAA and upscaler jitter, the shaders' noise and the probe rays
  followed the renderer's count of all frames, so a capture after a wait in real time could get another noise phase
  (the demo's tunnel motion capture failed now and then). These sequences now restart at every camera cut
  (`View::cut`, or the renderer's own): 13 runs of the native batch, 3 of them beside 14 busy processes, gave the
  same captures byte for byte.
- **Xcode builds put changed assets into the app one build late:** the merged `assets/` folder was a folder
  reference, which Xcode checks before the build that changes it. A script phase after the target's other phases
  now copies the changed files into the bundle and signs it again when it copied something (with Xcode's identity
  and entitlements); a texture changed and changed back reached the app in the build that cooked it, with a valid
  signature ([App shell](../app.md#ios-runs)).
- **The demo's `menu.txt` in the compact layout:** its clicks and touches on the section list failed on phones and
  in the iOS Simulator (7 of 39), where the list is a sheet; the scenario now opens the sheet first (35 of 35).
  Scripted gamepad input works in the Simulator now (hello's `input.txt` 14 of 14, the demo's `physics.txt` 18 of
  18 and `input.txt` 22 of 22).

## What was not verified

- **On a phone:** since 0.9.0 the iPhone 17 ran the tunnel, the ten-minute run, ASTC textures and asset hot reload,
  nothing else of this release: not the device test app (its last run there had the stage 11 code), not Vorbis,
  the reverb or the HRTF, the animation additions, normal maps in ray hits, the probe grid in turns, shadow maps
  for local lights, GTAO or SSR (their phone scenarios ran only on the Mac). Asset hot reload ran on SDL GPU only.
  The iPhone 18 Pro Max has run neither the stage 11 nor the stage 12 code.
- **By eye and ear:** nobody has looked at the new features in motion (the area lights, the brushed aluminium and
  the puddle, the tunnel's motion, 2D blend spaces, animation LOD, dual quaternions, the retargeted walk, blend
  shapes, the probe grid in turns, shadow maps, GTAO, SSR) or listened to Vorbis, the reverb or the HRTF; the
  demo's check mode lists the tunnel's, the animation's and the audio's. Still captures were looked at on the Mac
  and from the iPhone 17.
- The tunnel's phone defaults do not hold 60 Hz on the iPhone 17 after about eight minutes of load; the probe
  grid's phone default (turns) is not chosen.
- Shader hot reload: not with the Xcode generator (no manifests), not on Windows or Linux, its cost per pipeline
  bind not measured on the screen. ASTC's fallback on a device without ASTC sampling has not run (every device here
  samples ASTC).
- Open: the footprint on Vulkan through MoltenVK still grows a little from round to round (within the bound);
  `--present-mode immediate` was paced in two runs on 2026-11-06 for an unknown reason; MetalFX denoising's `bloom`
  range reads 2.45 ms in one region per run in a window, not explained.
- Carried over: HDR output on an HDR display, ray tracing on Vulkan (MoltenVK has none) and the DXIL of the ray
  query entries (not built on the Mac).

## Changes for games

What a game on 0.9.0 changes or checks when it moves to 0.10.0. Neither game uses `quench_render`, the asset
system, the engine's audio or its animation yet.

- **Ray hits read normal maps:** with traced reflections, indirect light, the probe grid or the path tracer, a
  material with a normal map (texture 2) looks different in reflections and bounced light than before (its normal
  map was ignored there); captures of such scenes change. The renderer makes three more pipelines when ray-traced
  lighting starts and the path tracer one more (`RenderStats::pipelines`).
- **Local light shadow maps** (all additions; nothing changes while `localShadowMapSize` is 0):
  `RenderSettings::localShadowMapSize` (after `localLightShadows`), `ActiveEffects::localShadowMaps`,
  `RenderStats::localShadowLights`, `localShadowMaps`, `localShadowInstances` and `localShadowDraws` (after
  `localShadowChanges`), `SurfaceDesc::localShadowFragmentShader` (after `basicFragmentShader`; designated
  initializers keep working). A surface shader of a game's own builds `surface_local_shadows_fs:fragment` and
  passes it there to get these shadows; without it its local lights stay unshadowed in frames with maps. Shader
  side: `FrameUniforms::localShadows` is appended (a `float4` after `rayLighting`), fragment texture and sampler 13
  of `Surface.hlsli` are taken (`quenchLocalShadowMaps`), `LocalLight::shape` of a point or spot light now carries
  its maps (x the first layer + 1, y the spot map's tangent, z the near plane) and `LocalLight::cone.y` the channel
  of its maps as of its traced shadow.
- **Screen-space ambient occlusion** (additions; nothing changes while a game does not ask for it):
  `render::AmbientOcclusion::ScreenSpace` after `RayTraced` (a `switch` over the enum without a `default` warns
  until it handles it), `RenderSettings::screenSpaceFallbacks` (after `occlusionStrength`; false), and
  `RenderStats::screenOcclusion` and `screenPrepassDraws` (before `renderWidth`). With GTAO the renderer makes three
  more pipelines (`RenderStats::pipelines`) the first time it runs. Surfaces read it as the traced occlusion
  (`FrameUniforms::options.w` bit 2, sampler 6): a surface on `Surface.hlsli` needs nothing.
- **Screen-space reflections** (additions; nothing changes while a game does not ask for it):
  `render::Reflections::ScreenSpace` after `RayTraced` (a `switch` over the enum without a `default` warns until it
  handles it); `RenderSettings::screenSpaceFallbacks` now also covers the reflections, so a game that set it for
  GTAO gets SSR where it asks for traced reflections on a device that cannot trace (and the fallback line says
  "screen space (SSR)" in place of "the sky's"); `RenderStats::screenReflections` (after `screenOcclusion`). With SSR
  the renderer makes two more pipelines the first time it runs and keeps a copy of the HDR image. Shader side:
  `FrameUniforms::options.w` bit 64 says sampler 7 holds screen-space reflections, whose alpha is a confidence that
  `Surface.hlsli` multiplies into the reflection's weight (bit 8 as before for the traced ones): a surface on
  `Surface.hlsli` needs nothing; one that reads sampler 7 itself should do the same.
- **`render::RenderSettings::probeUpdatePeriod`** is new (after `probeSpacing`; default 1, as before).
  `RenderStats::probes` is the probes traced this frame: 2048 as before by default, 2048 / n in turns. A shader of
  a game's own that reads the probe atlases through `quenchProbeIrradiance` needs nothing; one that reads the
  irradiance atlas directly should weight a probe by its alpha (0: no value yet).
- **`render::LightType`** has `Rect`, `Disk` and `Tube` after `Spot`: a `switch` over it without a `default` warns
  (`-Wswitch`) until it handles them.
- **`render::LightDesc`** has new members after `sourceRadius`: `size` (`glm::vec2`), `tangent` (`glm::vec3`),
  `twoSided`. Designated initializers in the old order compile unchanged. For an area light `color` times
  `intensity` is the radiance of its surface, not an irradiance; `direction` is a rect's or disk's normal and
  `range` is measured from the shape.
- **Area lights draw their shape:** the scene adds an instance, a material and a mesh for each one, outside the
  scene's handles and counts; `RenderStats::draws` includes them. At most 8 of a frame's 16 lights are area lights
  (the nearest).
- **`render::MaterialDesc`** has `anisotropy` and `anisotropyRotation` after `name` (default 0: isotropic, as
  before). Anisotropy needs vertex tangents (`computeTangents` or cooked models).
- **`render::RenderStats::areaLights`** and **`localShadowChanges`** are new (`localShadowChanges` before
  `renderWidth`: designated initializers in the old order still compile; a positional aggregate initialization
  would fill the members after it one off); `diskMesh` and `cylinderMesh` are new in `MeshBuilder.h`.
- **Shaders built on `quench/Surface.hlsli`:** `MaterialUniforms` gained `float4 shading` at its end (x the
  anisotropy, yz its rotation, w the area light shapes); `LocalLight` gained `float4 shape`, its `cone` and
  `direction` carry area light values, and `LightUniforms::count.y` counts the area lights. The fragment sampler 12
  holds the area lights' table: a custom surface must not use it. `QuenchSurface` has two new fields,
  `anisotropy` and `anisotropyDirection`: a custom surface that fills a `QuenchSurface` itself sets `anisotropy` to
  0 (or its own value) and `anisotropyDirection` to its tangent. A custom surface gets the area lights without
  changes. It also builds `surface_basic_fs:fragment` and passes it as **`SurfaceDesc::basicFragmentShader`**
  (new, after `fragmentShader`) to skip those paths where a frame does not need them; `QUENCH_SURFACE_FULL` is 0
  in that entry, for code of its own around area lights or anisotropy. Without it nothing changes.
- **A camera cut restarts the jitter and noise sequences** (`View::cut`, and the renderer's own cuts: a new size, a
  jump of more than 6 m, a turn of more than about 25 degrees): a game's frames right after a cut have the same
  jitter and noise as after any other cut. A game that sets `cut` every frame gets the same noise every frame.
- **`app::PerfReport::gpuSamples`** (and the perf line's sample count) is the number of frames whose GPU time was
  read, a few less than `frames` (the frames in flight at the end), where it equalled `frames` before; GPU times of
  a region come only from its own frames. **`PerfReport::processCpu`** and **`cpuPerformanceShare`** are new, at its
  end. The `perf:` line has `, process <ms> per frame (<n>% on performance cores)` after the CPU percentiles on
  Apple platforms and then `; wait ms p50 <ms> p95 <ms> (<present mode>, display <n> Hz)` everywhere: a parser of
  the line that expects `; gpu ms` right after `p99 <ms>` needs to allow both. New values `perf_wait_p50`,
  `perf_wait_p95`, `perf_present_mode`, `perf_display_hz`; **`gpu::Device::presentMode(window)`** is new.
- No new dependency library in the programs: the LTC data is code and a table in `quench_render` (BSD-2-Clause,
  with the paper reference its licence asks for), the HRTF data a table in `quench_audio` (Apache-2.0: a game that
  ships `quench_audio` ships the licence text from `THIRD_PARTY_LICENSES`), the GTAO pass code in `quench_render`
  (MIT). The cook adds astc-encoder (fetched at every host configure with the cook's other libraries, a 35 MB
  archive).
- **iOS builds cook ASTC 4x4 instead of BC7:** `quench_cook_assets()` without `TEXTURE_FORMAT` (or with `auto`)
  now cooks ASTC 4x4 for iOS (`CMAKE_SYSTEM_NAME` iOS) and BC7 elsewhere, as before; `TEXTURE_FORMAT bc7` keeps
  BC7 everywhere. Every iPhone that runs iOS 26 samples ASTC. A game that reads a texture's format
  (`TextureAsset::format()`) on iOS sees `Astc4x4Srgb` or `Astc4x4` there; a game that ships ASTC files to a device
  without ASTC sampling gets the placeholder and an error (BC7 is still decoded on the CPU there).
- **`cook::TextureFormat`** (the cook library, `tools/cook/Cook.h`) has `Astc4x4` after `Rgba8`; the cook's
  `--format` takes `astc`.
- **Debug builds read `asset_overlay/` in the user directory first:** a file at `asset_overlay/<path>` replaces the
  asset `<path>` in builds without `NDEBUG` (on the Mac too). A game that writes files of its own there, or wants
  Debug builds to read only the bundle, sets `-DQUENCH_ASSET_OVERLAY=OFF`. **`fs::makeOverlayBackend`** is new.
- **`ShaderBlob::manifest`** is a new member at the end of `ShaderBlob` (filled by the generated tables). Code that
  builds a `ShaderBlob` itself with a positional initializer warns under `-Wmissing-field-initializers` until it
  adds it. **`--hot-reload` now reloads shaders too** on desktops (the build's sources); `quench_add_shaders()`
  writes `quench_shaders/<set>/manifest.json` in host builds with a single-configuration generator.
- **`.ogg` files play:** a sound or stream that is an Ogg Vorbis file decoded to `Failed` before and plays now. The
  decoder's error for a file of no known format reads "not a WAV, FLAC, MP3 or Ogg Vorbis file". `quench_miniaudio`
  (for a game that links it) is built from the engine's `src/audio/Miniaudio.c` with stb_vorbis inside, instead of
  `miniaudio.c`; its configuration is unchanged. It also holds verblib's implementation (`verblib_*` symbols).
- **`audio::PlayParams`** has `sendBus` and `sendLevel` at its end (default: no send), **`audio::BusDesc`** and
  **`audio::BusInfo`** have `reverb` (`std::optional<ReverbDesc>`) at theirs: designated initializers and positional
  ones that stop before them compile unchanged. **`audio::ReverbDesc`**, **`AudioSystem::setSend`** and
  **`AudioSystem::setReverb`** are new. Without a reverb bus and sends the mix is bit for bit what it was.
- **`audio::AudioSettings`** has `hrtf` (off) and `hrtfVoices` (8) at its end; **`AudioSystem::setHrtf`** and
  **`hrtf()`** are new. With the HRTF off the mix is bit for bit what it was, but an `AudioSystem` with
  `hrtfVoices` above 0 (the default) keeps the table as floats and the filters, about 440 KB at 48 kHz: set
  `hrtfVoices = 0` to leave them out (then `setHrtf(true)` warns and stays off). Panned 3D voices cost about 8 % more
  on the audio thread (about 0.2 µs per millisecond of audio for 32 voices on the M4 Pro).
- **`audio::AudioStats::hrtfVoices`** is new, after `maxVoices`: designated initializers compile unchanged, a
  positional aggregate initialization would fill the members after it one off. New scenario value
  `audio_hrtf_voices`.
- **Xcode projects** (`quench_add_game` with the Xcode generator): the merged assets are no longer a folder
  reference in the target's resources but are copied by a `CMake PostBuild Rules` script phase; a game's own script
  that expects the folder in `Copy Bundle Resources` needs to change. The `<target>_assets` target stays.
- **`animation::StateDef`** has `blend2d` and `blend2dParameters` after `blendParameter`, and
  **`animation::BlendPoint`** has `phase` at its end: designated initializers compile unchanged, a positional
  aggregate initialization of `StateDef` would fill the members after them one off. **`animation::BlendPoint2D`**,
  **`kMaxBlendPoints`** and **`blendSpaceWeights()`** are new.
- **Graphs:** a 1D blend tree takes at most 16 clips (`kMaxBlendPoints`; before, any number), and a state with both
  `clip` and `blend2d`, or `blend` and `blend2d`, is refused by `parseGraph`.
- **Animation LOD:** **`animation::AnimationLod`**, **`AnimationSettings::lod`** (at its end; off),
  **`AnimationSystem::setLod`**, **`lod()`**, **`setLodView`** and **`clearLodView`** are new;
  **`CharacterDesc::height`** (1.8 m, at its end), **`CharacterState::lodRate`**, **`lodIk`** and **`poseChanged`**
  and **`AnimationStats::evaluated`** and **`lodRates`** (at their ends) are new. Nothing changes until a game turns
  it on.
- **`render::Scene::setJointPalette`** with the matrices the instance already has (and no `teleport`) is ignored:
  the instance no longer counts as moved (`RenderStats::movedInstances`) for it. An unchanged palette's vertices are
  copied from the frame before instead of skinned again; **`RenderStats::skinnedReused`** is new, after
  `skinnedVertices` (designated initializers compile unchanged, a positional aggregate initialization would fill the
  members after it one off).
- **`render::InstanceDesc`** has `skinning` at its end (`render::Skinning::Linear`, as before): designated
  initializers compile unchanged. **`render::Skinning`**, **`Scene::setSkinning`** and **`SceneAssets::setSkinning`**
  are new. A game's own compute shader that reads the renderer's palette buffer gets dual quaternions for instances
  that use them (`SkinUniforms::counts.w` 1); nothing changes for instances left linear.
- **`quench_cook_assets`** takes `RETARGET` (needs `SKELETON`); calls without it cook as before. In the cook library
  (`tools/cook/Cook.h`), **`cook::AnimationOptions`** has `retarget` at its end, and `RetargetMap`,
  `parseRetargetMap` and `retargetClips` are new.
- **`.qmodel` version 4:** the cook writes version 4 (blend shapes); this engine still reads version 3 files (as
  models without blend shapes), but an engine before this release cannot read the new files: a game that ships
  cooked models cooks them with the engine it runs. **`assets::ModelMesh::morphTargets`**,
  **`assets::ModelMorphTarget`** and **`assets::ModelMorphVertex`** are new; `writeModel` refuses more than 256
  targets per mesh or a target with an offset count other than the vertex count.
- **Meshes with glTF morph targets cook differently:** such a mesh becomes a part of its own per node (before, its
  primitives were merged with the others of the same material and skin, and the targets were ignored), with its
  vertices welded with their offsets. Models without morph targets cook to the same meshes as before.
- **`render::MeshDesc`** has `morphs` and `morphTargetCount` at its end, **`render::MorphVertex`**,
  **`Scene::setMorphWeights`** and **`Scene::morphTargetCount`** are new: designated initializers compile
  unchanged. A mesh with blend shapes goes through the skinning pass even without joints
  (`RenderStats::skinnedInstances` counts it).
- **`render::ModelAsset::Part`** has `morphs` and `morphDefaults` at its end; **`ModelAsset::morphTargets()`**,
  **`morphDefaults()`** and **`SceneAssets::setMorphWeights`** are new. A model's parts show the file's default
  weights until the game sets them.
- **The skinning pass's buffers** (for a game's own compute shader that reads them): the palette buffer holds an
  instance's (target, weight) rows in front of its joint rows (`SkinUniforms::counts.y` is still its first joint
  row), `SkinUniforms::morph` is new (the count of those rows, the first of them, 1 when the mesh has joints), and
  instances with weights are posed by a second entry, `skin_morph_cs`, which reads a fourth storage buffer, the
  mesh's morph offsets. Skinned meshes without weights cost what they did (within 2.5 % on the demo's crowd).
- **The cook library** links fastgltf's simdjson (for glTF extras); `cook::loadGltf` takes an optional
  `GltfExtras*`, and `cook::morphTargetName` is new. `quench_cook model --verbose` prints the blend shape count
  before the byte count.
- **`.qanim` version 2:** a clip set with float channels is written as version 2, which an engine before this
  release cannot read; one without channels is still written as version 1, byte for byte as before (clips cooked
  from glTF files without `weights` animations, and every retargeted clip set of such files). glTF `weights`
  animations, which were not cooked before, now cook into channels: a game whose files have them gets version 2 clip
  sets. In a file without skins a node animated only by weights is now a joint of the skeleton, as any animated
  node.
- **`animation::ClipDesc`** has `channels` at its end and **`animation::ChannelTrack`** is new;
  **`ClipBuildOptions`** has `channelTolerance` before `optimize` (designated initializers compile unchanged, a
  positional aggregate initialization would put `optimize` into it); **`Clip::channels()`**,
  **`AnimationSystem::addChannels`** and **`AnimationSystem::channels`** are new. Characters without channel sets
  update as before.
