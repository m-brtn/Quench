# Quench 0.20.0

2026-11-14. Four additions asked for by the first game being built on the engine from scratch (an isometric action
RPG inside a space station): an entity component system, many local lights, a game interface kit and decals, each
with a section in the demo (16 to 19). Changes since [0.19.1](v0.19.1.md). Source only.

**Released without the full gate, by the user's decision** ("without tests"): the checks that ran are the targeted
ones listed below, on the Mac, in Release. Read "What was not verified" before relying on it elsewhere.

## What changed

- **`quench_ecs`: an entity component system** ([ECS](../ecs.md)). Optional and headless; no other module links
  it. It is Flecs 4.2.0 with the engine around it: `ecs::World::fixedStep(dt)` runs the systems made with
  `.kind<ecs::FixedStep>()`, `update(dt)` every other system; Flecs's messages go to the engine's log; Flecs starts
  no thread (its thread functions are taken out); counters for a panel. Flecs is built from its two distribution
  files without its HTTP server, REST, script, reflection, JSON and statistics addons.
- **64 local lights a frame, shaded by screen tiles** ([Render](../render.md#many-lights)). A frame takes the 64
  nearest local lights (16 before; still at most 8 area lights and 4 with shadows). The render target is laid out
  in 20 x 12 tiles; each tile gets the set of lights whose reach touches it, and a surface shades only those. The
  sets ride in the light uniforms, so no backend and no surface shader needs a new binding.
  `RenderSettings::lightTiles` (on) switches it off to compare; `RenderStats::lightTileRefs` counts lights per
  tile. Ray hits and the path tracer use the nearest 16, the lights they used before.
- **`quench_ui`: a game's interface** ([Game UI](../ui.md)). `ui::Canvas` draws text in the game's fonts (files
  merged into one font, so a Cyrillic subset sits beside a Latin one), shapes, pictures, a bar, a speech bubble,
  halftone dots and a sticker's backing, and turns, scales and fades whatever is drawn inside a transform scope.
  `ui::Ui` adds what takes input: pressable areas, buttons, a pane, tips, slots with drag and drop, a touch stick
  and buttons, a focus for the gamepad or the keys, timers for things that pop in, named points for scenarios. It
  draws through Dear ImGui's draw lists, which the app already renders everywhere; ImGui stays the tool for debug
  panels.
- **Decals** ([Render](../render.md#decals)). `render::Decals` lays lit marks on flat surfaces: six shapes made by
  the engine (disc, ring, splat, scorch, square, stripes) or a texture of the game's, tinted, turned, with a life
  and a fade, at most 256 with the oldest replaced. They are flat quads on a plane the game names: they do not
  wrap around edges or follow curved geometry.
- **The demo** has 19 sections: 16 `ecs` (a swarm of 600 entities whose systems can be switched off while they
  run), 17 `lights` (up to 96 lamps in a dark hall), 18 `hud` (a comic HUD: meters, a radio line with a sticker
  face and a bubble, an inventory), 19 `decals` (painted marks and the marks of a fight; a click stamps one).

## Changes for games

Nothing a game has to change: no API was removed or renamed.

- `QUENCH_MAX_LIGHTS` is 64 and `shader::LightUniforms` grew (a set per tile, `count.z`): a game that fills or
  reads that struct itself (none does) must follow. A game's surface shaders need no change.
- A scene with more than 16 local lights in view now lights with up to 64 of them: such a scene gets brighter and
  costs more than it did.
- New targets to link when wanted: `quench_ecs`, `quench_ui`. `render::Decals` is in `quench_render`.
- The first configure fetches one more dependency, Flecs (MIT; `THIRD_PARTY_LICENSES`).
- The game skill (`.agents/skills/quench-game/`) has three new bullets (entities, the interface, decals): copy it
  again when the pin moves.

## What was verified

From the [2026-11-14 devlog](../../devlog/2026-11-14.md), on the Mac (Apple M4 Pro), Release only:

- **ECS:** `quench_ecs_tests`, 5 cases, passed.
- **Render:** `ctest -L render -LE vulkan`, 184 cases on SDL GPU's Metal driver and the native Metal backend,
  passed, with the two new ones also on Vulkan through MoltenVK: `renderer_many_lights_by_screen_tiles` (40 lamps:
  the picture with tiles equal to the one without, pixel for pixel; 70 lights in view, 64 used) and
  `renderer_decals_mark_fade_and_recycle`.
- **The demo:** the three batches (SDL GPU's Metal driver, the native Metal backend, Vulkan through MoltenVK) and
  all 141 of their parts passed in the last run: every earlier golden image is unchanged, the four new sections'
  captures are the same on the three backends, and `demo_ui.png` was replaced (the menu lists 19 sections).
  `demo_cycle_vulkan` failed in 2 of the day's earlier runs of its batch and passed when run again; its failed
  check was not captured.
- **Cost of the lights** (section 17, 64 lamps standing still, 1920 x 1080 offscreen, native Metal, median GPU
  time of a frame): 8.0 ms with tiles and 13.4 ms without at a reach of 4 m; 10.3 ms and 13.6 ms at 8 m.
- **The browser build** (`web` preset) compiles and links with all four additions; the publishing script's own
  check of the page is in the deployment note below.
- **A game on it:** the game these additions were made for builds against this code and passes its three scripted
  checks (its ECS world, its lamps and decals, its HUD and touch controls on `quench_ui`); its author played an
  early build of it on the Mac with a mouse and a gamepad.
- `tools/format.sh --check` and `tools/check_skill.py`: clean.

## What was not verified

- **The gate was not run:** not `tools/check.sh` in any mode; not the Debug build (so none of the new
  `QUENCH_ASSERT`s ran), ASan, TSan, the headless tests as WebAssembly under Node.js (a headless module was added),
  the iOS Simulator, the Xcode project, a new game from the template, `demo_tour`, `demo_benchmark`, `demo_checks`,
  the demo's package. The release CTest as a whole was not run: only the labels and parts named above.
- **Nothing was looked at on the screen by the agent**, and the user has not yet looked at the four demo
  sections: no motion, no real mouse, finger or gamepad on `quench_ui` in the demo, no phone, no Windows.
- **Lights:** the frame without lamps was not measured, so the cost of the lights themselves is not known; 8 ms at
  1080p is a lot for a phone; no depth slices (a long corridor seen along its length gains less).
- **UI:** no layout engine, scrolling, text input or sliders; an outlined text is drawn nine times; a screen of
  two pixels a point was not tried.
- **Decals:** flat only; flicker against the surface at grazing angles and far away was not examined; they are
  not in the ray tracing structures.
- **ECS:** no worker threads for systems, no reflection or saving of a world; the cost of a step was not recorded.
- `quench_ui` has no tests of its own (it needs the app's ImGui context): the demo's part is its check.
