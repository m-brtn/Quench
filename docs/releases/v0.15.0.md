# Quench 0.15.0

2026-11-10. Phones in a browser: an opt-in reaction to frames that come late (a lower render scale while a device
does not keep up), `App::mobile()` in a phone's or tablet's browser, and a frame rate limit that keeps a browser's
frames on the display's refreshes. Asked for after the Outpost micro game ran at 30 to 40 frames per second in an
iPhone's browser and warmed the phone (user report). Changes since [0.14.0](v0.14.0.md). Source only.

**Nothing of this release has run on a phone.** The gates ran on the release's code before its version number and
documentation (below); nothing was run again on the tagged commit (the user's decision, 2026-11-10).

## What changed

- **The reaction to late frames** (`App::loadGovernor()`, `include/quench/app/LoadGovernor.h`; opt-in). The App
  feeds it every frame on the screen that runs in real time: its time, the main thread's and, where known, the
  GPU's. A window of a second with at least a quarter of its frames late (more than 1.25 intervals of the frame
  rate to hold) takes the factor for the render scale one step down (x0.85; two steps when the mean frame took more
  than 1.75 intervals), to x0.5 at the lowest. It holds while the main thread alone takes more than 0.8 of the
  interval. After 10 s without a late window it takes a step back up: where the GPU's time is known only if the
  frame would still fit, elsewhere as a try, and a try that brings late frames back doubles the wait for the next
  (up to 160 s). The game applies `loadGovernor().renderScale(playerScale)` to what its renderer gets; the engine
  changes no setting. It needs no thermal state, which a browser does not report.
  [App shell](../app.md#frame-load).
- **`App::mobile()` in a browser.** True in a web build on a phone or tablet (the user agent, `userAgentData`,
  a Mac's user agent with a touch screen, or a touch-only pointer), so a game's defaults for phones apply there;
  it was false in every browser. The flag `--mobile` makes it true on any device, to try those defaults on a
  desktop.
- **A frame rate limit in a browser** (`App::setMaxFrameRate`, `--max-frame-rate`, the thermal reaction's) keeps
  the frames on `requestAnimationFrame`: the app lets early refreshes pass without a frame, as on iOS. Before, the
  limit moved SDL's callbacks onto a timer.
- **The interval a frame is judged against** is a whole number of the display's refreshes (60 Hz on a 144 Hz
  display: every third one). A browser tells no refresh rate: there the app estimates it from the cadence of the
  browser's callbacks (the highest rate of the last ten seconds, at least 60 Hz, a common rate within 4%).
- **Scenarios:** the command `frame_load FRAME_MS [CPU_MS [GPU_MS]]` (fake frame times for the reaction, also
  offscreen; `frame_load system` ends it) and the values `load_scale`, `load_changes`, `load_raise_seconds`,
  `perf_load_scale` and `mobile`; a `perf:` line names the factor while the reaction is on.
- **The demo:** Adapt to load in Settings (off; `adapt_to_load` in `settings.json`), its line in the Performance
  panel, the part `load` in the batches. [Engine demo](../demo.md).
- **Outpost** (`games/outpost`, after the tag): a low detail (render scale 0.67, shadow maps of 2048 with 4 taps,
  no screen-space occlusion, no lamps by day) that is the default where `App::mobile()`, with at most 60 frames per
  second there, and the reaction on. From a measurement of its frame at a phone's size:
  [Performance](../performance.md#a-games-frame-at-a-phones-size).

## What was verified

All on the Mac (Apple M4 Pro), offscreen.

- **`tools/check.sh full`** (22:00 to 22:11, passed, on this release's code before the version number, the
  documentation and the notes): format; Debug, ASan and TSan 689 tests each, 70 skipped; Release 832, 69 skipped,
  on SDL GPU's Metal driver, the native Metal backend and Vulkan through MoltenVK (the demo's batches 806, 819 and
  806 checks; every golden capture within the thresholds, none changed); `wasm-node` 205 under Node.js, 9 skipped;
  the iOS Simulator 523, 70 skipped; the Xcode project generated; a new game built and started.
- **`tools/check.sh step web`** (21:50, passed, the same code): in headless Chrome on the Mac hello's check 21
  passed, the demo's batch 806 passed, 0 failed, no WebGPU error, 41 captures within the thresholds of the Mac's
  Metal golden images.
- **The reaction:** 9 unit cases of the policy and 2 of the refresh estimate; the demo's `load` part, 44 checks on
  each of the Mac's three backends, with faked frames (one step a second, two for frames of 40 ms, the floor, a
  busy main thread holds, the step up, the doubled wait after a failed try, no step up that the GPU time forbids).
- **A browser's limit and `mobile()`** (Outpost's page in headless Chrome on the Mac, the path of a person's
  page): at a limit of 60 Hz 480 frames in 8.0 s, at 30 Hz 181 in 6.0 s, without one 359 in 6.0 s; with an
  iPhone's user agent given to Chrome the app logs a phone.
- **Outpost's frame** (native Metal backend, 2622 x 1206, GPU busy time p50): the defaults 4.38 to 4.45 ms, of
  which the main pass 2.29; the low detail 1.65 to 1.74 ms. As a page in headless Chrome on the Mac the main thread
  takes 0.49 ms a frame: the GPU's work per pixel limits this game, not the WebAssembly side.

## What was not verified

- **Nothing on a phone or in a phone's browser:** whether the low detail and the reaction bring an iPhone to 60
  frames per second, how warm it gets, how a phone's browser paces animation frames when the GPU is the limit, the
  detection of phones' and tablets' browsers on real devices.
- **The reaction's thresholds were chosen, not tuned:** it was checked with faked frame times and in runs on the
  Mac whose frames are never late. Nobody has watched a step of the render scale on a screen.
- **Nothing ran on the tagged commit** (the user's decision): the gates above ran before the version number
  changed. No packaged demo for this release; the Windows workflow was not started.
- The demo's CPU cost in a browser (weather, particles on the CPU, navigation) was not measured; whether threads,
  SIMD or a narrower Asyncify matter there is open.

## Changes for games

Nothing has to change. Behaviour that differs:

- `App::mobile()` is true in a web build on a phone or tablet: a game that chooses defaults by it gets them there.
- `--mobile` is an engine flag now: a game with an argument of that name of its own no longer receives it.
- The scenario command `frame_load` and the values `load_scale`, `load_changes`, `load_raise_seconds`,
  `perf_load_scale` and `mobile` are the engine's: a game's of the same names are no longer asked.
- In a web build a frame rate limit paces frames by the display's refreshes instead of a timer.

TrailForge (pinned `d708783`) and Ashfall (its own code) are not affected until they move; neither uses these
names.
