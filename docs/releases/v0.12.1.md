# Quench 0.12.1

2026-11-09. Fixes for wide light trails attached to jittering points and sunlight shadows from the HDR demo's
glowing panels. Changes since [0.12.0](v0.12.0.md). Source only. The trail fix ran offscreen on the Mac and in the
iOS Simulator; headless modules also ran as WebAssembly under Node.js. The later HDR demo correction was checked
on the Mac's three backends. The patch has not been run on an iPhone.

## What changed

- A trail's two neighbouring segments now compute exactly the same frame at their shared endpoint. Previously,
  when the projected direction collapsed, the fallback depended on the segment being drawn; the shared endpoint
  could swap sides and form sharp wedges.
- The screen-facing direction is estimated over the ribbon's width, using at most eight sampled neighbours in
  either direction. Small oscillations of an attachment no longer dictate the orientation of a wide soft ribbon.
  The search stays inside the current run and in front of the near plane.
- Degenerate directions fade out instead of receiving full visibility. Tight turns limit the join's width and
  taper a reversal to a point. The recorded head positions, trail lifetime, centreline and authored colors stay
  unchanged; ordinary broad ribbons retain their width.
- A rendering regression covers centimetre-scale emitter jitter on a 0.9 m translucent trail, three views close
  to its direction, and a side view that must retain the broad glow. The existing straight-trail, end-on fade,
  lifetime and sparkle checks remain.
- The HDR demo's opaque glowing panels now cast sunlight shadows. Changing their brightness or display-output
  controls keeps those shadows enabled; previously the panel material explicitly disabled shadow casting.

### Trail cost

Native Metal on the M4 Pro, 1920 x 1080 offscreen, 20 trails with about 8,800 strip vertices and 1,715 particles;
two four-second before/after pairs in opposite order, nominal thermal state. The `particles` GPU draw range p50
rose from 0.134–0.136 ms to 0.142 ms (0.006–0.008 ms, about 4–6%). The bounded extra vertex work is accepted to
remove the spikes while retaining wide trails.

CPU p50 was 0.366–0.378 ms before and 0.404–0.436 ms after (0.026–0.070 ms higher); this is not reported as zero
CPU regression. Median frame time stayed around 0.88 ms. The second after-run had a 1.013 ms mean and 2.521 ms p95
against 0.918 ms and 1.068 ms in its before-run. The user's demo was active during that second pair, so its wall-time
and tail differences are not an isolated estimate of this patch's cost. These are offscreen measurements, not
display-rate or phone performance claims; [the devlog](../../devlog/2026-11-09.md) records both pairs.

## What was not verified

- No iPhone run, phone cost measurement or onscreen performance benchmark has been performed for this patch.
  Vulkan on the Mac means Vulkan through MoltenVK.

## Known issue carried forward

The intermittent Vulkan-through-MoltenVK layout validation error documented in [0.12.0](v0.12.0.md#known-issues)
recurred once in the full Release demo batch: `VUID-vkCmdDraw-None-09600` reported the cloud-layer noise image in
`TRANSFER_SRC_OPTIMAL` when shader sampling expected `SHADER_READ_ONLY_OPTIMAL`. It occurred in Weather, before
the Particles section. The isolated demo batch and all 41 dependent checks passed on retry. The cause remains
unresolved; this patch does not claim to fix it. MoltenVK is a translation layer over Metal, not a native Vulkan
driver.

## Changes for games

No public API, asset-format, settings or dependency changes. Rebuild the engine's render shaders when moving the
engine pin to 0.12.1. The appearance of a trail can change around tight turns or when viewed along its direction;
review affected golden captures deliberately. The fix is in ribbon rendering, so games keep their existing
`TrailDesc` and `Scene::moveTrail` calls. Attachment smoothing is still a game-specific visual choice.
