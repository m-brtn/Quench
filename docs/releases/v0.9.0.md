# Quench 0.9.0

2026-11-06. Stage 11 of the [roadmap](../roadmap.md): full ray tracing. Ray-traced shadows of local lights,
reflections and indirect light (a probe grid and per-pixel rays), the engine's temporal denoiser, MetalFX's
denoising upscaler, a reference path tracer, top-level refits and bottom-level compaction, and normal and emissive
maps with MikkTSpace tangents in the vertex. Source only. Changes since [0.8.0](v0.8.0.md) (tag `v0.8.0`). Ray
tracing runs on the native Metal backend (`--gpu-backend metal`) on GPUs that have it; every new effect is off by
default and falls back by `gpu::Caps`. The stage ran on the Mac; the same day the device tests and the costs of the
new effects followed on an iPhone 17 (A19), and the demo's ray tracing section got phone defaults from them, with
the system's thermal state and a frame rate limit in the app. The iPhone 18 Pro Max has not run the stage 11 code
(see "What was not verified"). Also in this release, from earlier the same day: the engine's Objective-C is built
with ARC in Xcode projects too (the native backend crashed on iOS devices in 0.8.0's Xcode builds), automated runs
are offscreen and take scripted input only, the checks come in tiers, and the demo has a Graphics panel and a
numbered menu.

## What changed

- **Normal and emissive maps** in the standard surface (texture slots 2 and 3), with **MikkTSpace tangents** in
  the vertex (user decision, 2026-11-06: the tangent space of glTF and of the bakers, the same frame for raster and
  rays). The vertex grew from 48 to 64 bytes (`render::Vertex::tangent`, `assets::ModelVertex::tangent`). `quench_cook`
  keeps a glTF file's `TANGENT` and generates MikkTSpace tangents where there is none (a vertex on a mirrored uv seam
  may be split); `.qmodel` is version 3. `assets::generateTangents` and `render::computeTangents` give tangents to
  meshes made elsewhere; `MeshBuilder`'s shapes come with them. `SceneAssets` maps a model's normal and emissive
  textures. mikktspace (zlib) is pinned like the other dependencies ([Jobs, assets and the cook](../assets.md)).
- **Ray-traced lighting** ([Render module](../render.md#ray-traced-lighting)): `RenderSettings::localLightShadows`
  (shadows of up to 4 point and spot lights with `castsShadows`, soft by `LightDesc::sourceRadius`),
  `reflections = Reflections::RayTraced` (one GGX ray per pixel up to `reflectionRoughness`),
  `indirectLight = IndirectLight::Probes` (a DDGI-like grid of 16 x 8 x 16 probes, `probeSpacing` apart, around
  `View::probeCenter` or the camera, 64 rays per probe per frame, with visibility) or `IndirectLight::RayTraced`
  (one ray per pixel over the grid). Hits read every instance's triangle and material, its base color,
  metallic-roughness and emissive textures (256 x 256 copies in texture arrays), and are shaded with the standard
  surface's BRDF. With them the ray tracing prepass also writes a G-buffer (normal, roughness, albedo, metallic),
  from a surface's new `surface_gbuffer_fs` or the standard surface.
- **Denoising** ([Render module](../render.md#denoising)): `RenderSettings::rayDenoiser`, `Temporal` by default (a
  history per traced signal, reprojected with motion vectors, clamped to the new samples' spread, cut short where it
  lags, then a-trous passes) or `Spatial` (stage 9's blurs); hard sun shadows without occlusion are not filtered by
  either. `Upscaling::TemporalDenoised` runs MetalFX's temporal
  denoised scaler on the raw signals in place of TAA and the engine's denoiser (`gpu::Caps::denoisedUpscaling`:
  macOS 26 and iOS 26 on GPUs that support it, the M4 Pro and the A20 Pro do; else `Temporal` with the engine's
  denoiser). User decision, 2026-11-06: both, the engine's for older systems, devices without MetalFX's, the TAA and
  FXAA paths and future backends.
- **Path tracer** ([Render module](../render.md#path-tracer)): `RenderSettings::pathTracer`, a progressive
  reference (next event estimation toward the sun and one local light, GGX and diffuse sampling, Russian roulette,
  `pathBounces`, `pathSamples`) that restarts on any change of the camera, the scene, a skinned pose or the
  settings; for comparing the real-time effects with, not for play.
- **Acceleration structures:** the top level is refitted while only transforms change (built again when instances
  or bottom levels change and after 30 refits); static bottom levels are compacted the frame after their build.
  `gpu::AccelerationStructurePass::refit(top, instances)`, `queryCompactedSize`, `copyAndCompact`,
  `gpu::Device::compactedSize`, `AccelerationStructureDesc::compactedSize`, `AccelerationStructure::bytes()`; the
  Metal backend handles counter-clockwise front faces through mirroring transforms (tested).
- **Statistics and timing:** `RenderStats::rayCompactions`, `rayTopRefit`, `rayMemoryBytes`, `hitTextures`,
  `probes`, `pathSamples`; GPU ranges `probes`, `reflections`, `indirect` inside `raytrace`, and `path`.
- **The demo's ray tracing section** ([Engine demo](../demo.md)) grew a framed mirror, five gold spheres of rising
  roughness, a red and green corner with a lamp, a spot behind the fence and an emissive panel; its defaults on the
  native backend are every stage 11 effect but per-pixel indirect light, with MetalFX temporal upscaling and the
  engine's temporal denoiser at render scale 0.67 where the device has MetalFX. MetalFX denoising is a choice in
  the panel: at 1080p on the M4 Pro it cost 6.2 ms against about 2 ms for MetalFX temporal with the engine's
  denoiser, so it is not the default (decided without the user). The Graphics panel has a "Ray-traced lighting"
  group and "MetalFX denoising"; five new check-mode items (29 in all); `raytracing_benchmark.txt` measures each
  new effect.
- **Phone defaults of the ray tracing section** (from the iPhone 17 runs, decided without the user): on phones and
  tablets that trace (`App::mobile()` and `gpu::Caps::rayTracing`) sun and lamp shadows, occlusion and reflections
  without the probe grid, render scale 0.5 (the user's plan allowed 0.67 or 0.5) with MetalFX spatial upscaling
  (temporal costs three times as much on the A19), and 60 frames per second while the section is current (a "60 Hz"
  box in its panel). `raytracing_phone.txt` measures each effect on a phone, each group of regions from the
  nominal thermal state; `raytracing_phone_sustained.txt` holds the defaults for a minute at the display's rate
  and at 60 Hz.
- **The system's thermal state** (`app::ThermalState`; Apple platforms: `NSProcessInfo`, unknown elsewhere):
  `FrameStats::thermal`, read every 0.5 s and logged on a change; the perf lines and `PerfReport` carry it for the
  region (start, end, highest); scenario values `thermal_state` and `perf_thermal_*`; the demo shows it in its
  Performance panel and About and writes it per section into `benchmark.json`. The engine does not react to it.
- **A frame rate limit:** `App::setMaxFrameRate(hz)` / `maxFrameRate()`, `--max-frame-rate HZ` and the scenario
  command `max_frame_rate`, in whole display refreshes; offscreen frames are not limited. `App::mobile()` tells
  phones and tablets.
- **Xcode builds copy re-cooked assets:** the merged `assets/` folder of a game's Xcode build (`quench_add_game`) is
  touched when a file in it changes; before, Xcode left a changed file in a subfolder out of the app (the demo ran
  on the iPhone with the mannequin in the old model format, and its ray tracing section never got ready). Xcode
  decides whether to copy when a build starts: build `<game>_assets` first, as `tools/package_demo.sh` does
  ([App shell](../app.md#ios-runs)).
- **ARC on every generator:** the engine's Objective-C and Objective-C++ sources are compiled with ARC by
  `quench_configure_target` (`-fobjc-arc` and Xcode's `CLANG_ENABLE_OBJC_ARC`). The flag used to sit behind a
  `$<COMPILE_LANGUAGE:OBJCXX>` generator expression, which the Xcode generator drops: every `ios-xcode` build (the
  iPhone test app and 0.8.0's `.ipa` from `tools/package_demo.sh --ios` among them) compiled the native Metal backend
  without ARC, and it crashed on the iPhone in its copy pass (`MetalDevice::beginCopyPass`). Ninja builds (the Mac,
  the iOS Simulator, `ios-device-release`) had ARC. The seven files that rely on it stop the build with an `#error`
  without it.
- **Offscreen automated runs** ([App shell](../app.md#offscreen-runs), user decision, 2026-11-06): on desktops, runs
  with `--script`, `--capture` or `--quit-after` show no window and take no focus (on macOS no Dock icon either);
  frames render into an offscreen texture that captures read. `--onscreen` shows the window (the benchmarks run so),
  `--offscreen` makes any run offscreen. Keyboard, mouse, touch and gamepad events from the system are dropped in
  these runs and in every scripted run, also on the screen and on phones: only the script's events and its virtual
  gamepad get through (`Input::restrictGamepad`). Offscreen runs mix offline unless `--audio-device` names a device.
  Interactive runs are unchanged.
- **Scenario parts and batches:** `include FILE` runs another scenario file as a part (its `time` starts at 0, its
  `quit` ends only the part, the runner logs a result line per part). The demo and hello are checked in one process
  per GPU backend (`tests/RunBatch.cmake`, `tests/CheckPart.cmake`), with captures compared in Release only; the
  demo's section golden images show the scene without its windows (`windows off`), and `demo_ui.png` checks the
  menu.
- **Checks in tiers** (user decision, 2026-11-06): `tools/check.sh quick` checks the format of the changed files,
  builds Release and runs the tests of the touched modules, without the Vulkan variants (`gpu_vulkan.*`,
  `render_vulkan.*`, the demo's and hello's `_vulkan` tests); `full` runs format, `debug`, `release`, `asan`, `tsan`,
  `wasm-node`, the iOS Simulator and a new game; `release` adds the demo's package. The tests that need a window on
  the screen run only with `QUENCH_WINDOW_TESTS=1`.
- **The demo's Graphics panel** (user request; G or the Graphics button in the menu): one panel for the rendering
  options of every section (anti-aliasing, render scale and upscaling, shadows, occlusion, bloom, tone curve and
  exposure, present mode and HDR, and since stage 11 the ray-traced lighting and MetalFX denoising), with what runs
  after the device's fallbacks and why; a value set there overrides every section until "Use section defaults" and
  is kept in `settings.json`. **The demo's menu numbers its sections 1 to 11** (user report: the labels showed the
  digit keys, 0 for the tenth and nothing for the eleventh); the keys are unchanged.

## What was not verified

- The iPhone 18 Pro Max has not run the stage 11 code (it was not available): its device tests, the stage 11 costs
  and the phone defaults there are pending; its last runs are the baseline and the ARC fix above, on the code before
  stage 11. There the native backend's two window cases failed once (a portrait drawable in a landscape window) and
  passed in the next run; not reproduced. On the iPhone 17 the 60 Hz defaults ran one minute; a ten-minute run is
  open.
- The Graphics panel has not been used by a person (the user tests it by hand, their decision): it is built and its
  button is in the menu's golden image, but no test sets its options, and it has not been looked at on the Mac or a
  phone.
- The window tests (`QUENCH_WINDOW_TESTS=1`) have not run on the Mac since automated runs became offscreen; they
  ran in the iPhone test app.
- Nobody has looked at the new effects in motion: the temporal denoiser's lag and noise, MetalFX denoising while the
  sun turns and the character walks, the reflections and the probe light (the check mode's new items). Still images
  were looked at on the Mac.
- Ray tracing on Vulkan (MoltenVK has none) and the DXIL of the new ray query entries (not built on the Mac).
- Carried over from 0.8.0: HDR output on an HDR display, audio by ear, animation in motion, the demo's compact layout
  and touch controls on a device.

## Changes for games

What a game on 0.8.0 changes or checks when it moves to 0.9.0. Neither game uses `quench_render` or `.qmodel` yet.

- **The engine vertex is 64 bytes:** `render::Vertex` and `assets::ModelVertex` gained `glm::vec4 tangent` after
  their 48 bytes (`static_assert`s changed). Code that builds vertices by aggregate or writes them byte by byte, and
  shaders that read the vertex buffer themselves, need the new member; `QuenchVertexIn` has it. Meshes with normal
  maps need tangents: `render::computeTangents(MeshData&)` (it may split vertices on mirrored uv seams and drop
  unused ones) or `assets::generateTangents`.
- **`.qmodel` version 3:** versions 1 and 2 are refused with an error that says to cook the source again;
  models cooked by `quench_cook_assets()` are cooked again by the next build (its outputs depend on `quench_cook`).
- **Shaders built on `quench/Surface.hlsli`:** `FrameUniforms` grew by four `float4`s (`probeGrid`, `probeCounts`,
  `probeAtlas`, `rayLighting`), and the fragment samplers 7 to 11 are taken by the ray-traced reflections, indirect
  light, local light shadows and the probe atlases (6 was already the ray mask): a custom surface must not use
  them. `FrameUniforms::options.w` has bits 4, 8, 16 and 32 for the new signals. Surfaces get the new lighting
  without changes; a custom surface may add `surface_gbuffer_fs` (from `quench/SurfaceEntries.hlsli`) and set
  `SurfaceDesc::gbufferShader` so traced reflections and indirect light see its own normal and roughness.
- **`render::RenderSettings`:** new `localLightShadows`, `reflections`, `reflectionRoughness`, `indirectLight`,
  `probeSpacing`, `rayDenoiser` (default `RayDenoiser::Temporal`: where stage 9's ray-traced shadows and occlusion
  ran with the spatial blur, they now run with the temporal filter; set `Spatial` for the old look), `pathTracer`,
  `pathBounces`, `pathSamples`; `Upscaling::TemporalDenoised`. `render::ActiveEffects` gained `localLightShadows`,
  `reflections`, `indirectLight`, `pathTracer`, `rayDenoiser`.
- **`render::LightDesc`:** `sourceRadius` (point and spot); `castsShadows` now also means ray-traced shadows for
  point and spot lights (default true, so with `localLightShadows` above 0 the nearest local lights cast shadows).
- **`render::View::probeCenter`** (optional; the camera when unset). A top-down camera should set the point it looks
  at.
- **`render::RenderStats`:** `rayCompactions`, `rayTopRefit`, `rayMemoryBytes` (now with the acceleration
  structures), `hitTextures`, `probes`, `pathSamples`.
- **`quench::gpu`:** `AccelerationStructureDesc::allowRefit` applies to top levels too and `compactedSize` is new;
  `AccelerationStructurePass::refit(top, instances)`, `queryCompactedSize`, `copyAndCompact`;
  `Device::compactedSize`; `AccelerationStructure::bytes()`; `UpscalerType::TemporalDenoised` with the
  `UpscalerDesc` formats of its inputs and the `UpscaleDesc` inputs `diffuseAlbedo`, `specularAlbedo`, `normal`,
  `roughness`; `UpscaleDesc::exposure` (temporal types; 1 keeps the old behaviour); `Caps::denoisedUpscaling`.
- New dependency: mikktspace (zlib licence) in `THIRD_PARTY_LICENSES`.
- **`quench::app`, additions only:** `ThermalState` and `thermalStateName`, `FrameStats::thermal`,
  `PerfReport::thermalStart`, `thermalEnd`, `thermalMax` (the perf line gains a `thermal ...` field before the
  game's details: a script that parses it by position sees one more field); `App::setMaxFrameRate`,
  `maxFrameRate`, `App::mobile()`; `--max-frame-rate`.
- **Automated runs are offscreen on desktops:** runs with `--script`, `--capture` or `--quit-after` show no window,
  take no input from real devices and mix offline unless `--audio-device` is given; a scripted run takes only the
  script's input also on the screen and on phones. `--onscreen` restores the window (needed by a benchmark that
  measures the display's pacing), `--offscreen` forces the mode. A game's smoke test no longer needs
  `--audio-device null`.
- **Scenarios:** `include FILE`; `Scenario::parse` takes an optional loader for it (existing calls compile
  unchanged). `Input::restrictGamepad` is new.
- **ARC:** the engine's Objective-C and Objective-C++ sources are compiled with ARC in any parent project and with
  any generator (with 0.8.0 the Xcode generator built them without ARC, and the native backend crashed on iOS
  devices). A game's own Objective-C sources are left as they were (`quench_add_game` does not change the game
  target's options).
- **iOS builds of a game (`quench_add_game`, Xcode generator):** the plain and cooked assets are merged into one
  `assets/` folder by a new `<game>_assets` target (before, plain `ASSETS` alone were a folder reference of the
  source directory). Build `<game>_assets` before the game's scheme when assets changed, or they reach the app one
  build later.
